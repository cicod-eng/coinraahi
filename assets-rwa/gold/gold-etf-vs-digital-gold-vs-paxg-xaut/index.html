Gold ETF vs Digital Gold vs PAXG/XAUT: What Do You Actually Own in India?

Last updated: 6 September 2026 · Rules and rates verified 6 September 2026

All four of these claim to give you gold exposure, but they are not the same asset under Indian law. A gold ETF is a SEBI-regulated fund holding physical gold in a custodian's vault; digital gold is a fractional claim on vaulted gold sold by fintech platforms; and PAXG and XAUT are crypto tokens that track gold but are taxed as crypto, not gold. That last distinction is the one most people miss: a tokenised gold purchase avoids the 3% GST of digital gold but then faces 30% flat tax and 1% TDS on any gain, instead of gold's 12.5% long-term rate. For most Indian buyers wanting long-term gold exposure, the gold ETF is the cleanest and best-regulated starting point.

The short version

  • Gold ETF: SEBI-regulated, holds real allocated gold, no GST on purchase, capital-gains tax.
  • Digital gold: fractional vaulted gold, 3% GST on purchase, not SEBI/RBI regulated.
  • PAXG / XAUT: gold-backed tokens, treated as virtual digital assets — 30% tax + 1% TDS.
  • Held over the long term, gold is taxed at 12.5%; a token is taxed at 30%.
  • PAXG/XAUT redemption needs hundreds of tokens and is effectively cross-border.

Gold ETF — the regulated baseline

A gold ETF is a mutual fund that holds physical gold of 99.5% purity (the fineness used for investment-grade gold) in a SEBI-approved vault, through a custodian and a trustee. When you buy a unit, you own a proportionate claim on that allocated gold, held and audited under SEBI rules. You buy and sell it in your demat account, in rupees, with a fraction of a gram as the smallest practical holding.

Its cost is an expense ratio — typically under 1% a year — rather than a purchase tax. There is no GST on buying a gold ETF, and no making charges, because there is no fabrication. That makes it, on a pure cost basis, one of the cheapest ways to hold gold in India.

Digital gold — fractional, but not SEBI-regulated

Digital gold platforms such as SafeGold, MMTC-PAMP and Augmont let you buy a fraction of a gram of gold that is physically stored in a partner vault. It is genuinely gold, and you can often convert a holding into physical coins or bars above a minimum threshold. But the product itself sits outside SEBI and RBI regulation — it is a fintech arrangement between you, the platform and its vault custodian.

Two practical differences from a gold ETF: digital gold attracts 3% GST on purchase (a one-time, non-creditable cost for an individual), and it has no annual expense ratio — instead the platform earns through a buy–sell spread. For small, recurring purchases it is convenient; for large, long-term holdings, the unregulated structure and the upfront 3% are the trade-offs to weigh.

PAXG and XAUT — tokenised gold

PAXG (Paxos Gold) and XAUT (Tether Gold) are tokens issued on a blockchain, each designed to represent one fine troy ounce of gold. PAXG is backed by allocated London Good Delivery bars held by Paxos, with monthly reserve attestations by KPMG; XAUT is issued by TG Commodities Limited and backed by gold held in Swiss vaults, with BDO Italia auditing the reserves. In both cases the gold is real and held by a third party — but what you hold is a token, not the metal.

The catch is in redemption. Converting the token into actual gold requires a large number of tokens — roughly 430 tokens for a full bar in both cases — and delivery for XAUT is in Switzerland. Fractional redemption exists through some partners, but for a typical Indian retail buyer, the practical reality is that you can sell the token for cash, not walk away with gold. That gap between "gold-backed token" and "gold you can actually take delivery of" is the heart of the matter.

What you actually own — the hierarchy

These four are often grouped together as "gold", but they sit on different rungs of ownership and protection:

  • Gold ETF — you own fund units, with the underlying gold held and audited under SEBI regulation and a registered custodian.
  • Digital gold — you own a contractual claim on vaulted gold held by a custodian, but the platform arrangement is not SEBI/RBI regulated.
  • PAXG / XAUT — you own a token issued by a private company; the gold sits behind a redemption process that is impractical at small scale and located abroad.

Regulation and redeemability move together. The more you can actually take delivery of the gold, the more the product behaves like gold; the more it behaves like a token, the more it gets taxed like a token — which is exactly where the next section leads.

The tax difference that surprises people

Here is the part most buyers do not notice until filing season. Under Indian law:

  • Physical gold, digital gold and (long-term) gold ETFs are capital assets. Held beyond the qualifying period, gains are taxed at 12.5% with no indexation; sold earlier, at your slab rate.
  • PAXG and XAUT are virtual digital assets. Any gain is taxed at a flat 30% (plus cess) under Section 115BBH, with no deductions, no loss set-off, and 1% TDS under Section 194S on qualifying transfers.

Consider the same ₹1,00,000 gain. As gold, it costs roughly ₹12,500 at the long-term rate. As a token, it costs ₹31,200. The token route avoids the 3% GST on the way in but gives up far more on the way out — and that is before you factor in the inability to easily take physical delivery. This is tax information, not advice; confirm the numbers for your assessment year with a professional. See our crypto tax guide for the full VDA rules.

Do not confuse "gold-backed" with "gold for tax purposes." A token that tracks gold is still a virtual digital asset in India. The 3% GST you save upfront can cost you 30% instead of 12.5% later.

How to choose

  • Long-term gold exposure, lowest friction: a gold ETF — regulated, no GST, 12.5% long-term rate.
  • Tiny, recurring purchases with optional physical delivery: digital gold — convenient, but factor in the 3% GST and lack of SEBI oversight.
  • Want gold exposure inside a crypto portfolio and accept 30% VDA tax: PAXG/XAUT — with eyes open about tax and redemption.
  • Want physical metal you can hold: none of these are ideal; consider physical gold directly, or digital gold converted to coins.

The deciding question is the same one that runs through all of CoinRaahi's asset guides: what do you actually own, and what happens when you try to get it out? Answer those two questions and the right option is usually obvious.

Frequently asked questions

Is digital gold regulated in India?

No. Unlike gold ETFs (SEBI) or gold held by banks (RBI), digital gold platforms are not regulated by SEBI or RBI. The gold is real and vaulted, but the product itself is a private arrangement.

Is there GST on a gold ETF?

No GST is charged on buying a gold ETF; you pay an annual expense ratio instead. Digital gold, by contrast, attracts 3% GST on purchase.

Are PAXG and XAUT taxed like gold or like crypto in India?

Like crypto. They are treated as virtual digital assets, so gains are taxed at 30% under Section 115BBH with 1% TDS under Section 194S, not at the 12.5% long-term capital-gains rate that applies to gold.

Can I redeem PAXG or XAUT for physical gold?

In theory, yes — but it takes roughly 430 tokens for a full bar, and delivery is effectively outside India (XAUT delivery is in Switzerland). For most retail buyers the practical route is to sell the token, not redeem it for metal.

Which is cheapest to hold for the long term?

A gold ETF is usually cheapest: no purchase GST, no making charges, and a sub-1% annual expense ratio. Digital gold carries 3% GST upfront, and tokenised gold carries the far heavier 30% tax on gains.

Is tokenised gold safer than a gold ETF?

Not in a way that benefits an Indian retail buyer. A gold ETF is SEBI-regulated with a custodian; a gold-backed token depends on a private issuer's reserves and redemption process, and it is taxed far more heavily.
