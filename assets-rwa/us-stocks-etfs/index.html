US Stocks & ETFs for Investors in India

Last updated: 6 September 2026

Indian investors can gain exposure to US companies through several routes, but those routes do not always provide the same legal ownership, custody structure, costs or exit options. A real share, an ETF unit, a fractional interest and a tokenised product may track similar markets while giving the holder very different rights. CoinRaahi focuses on those differences.

Ways to access US markets

  • International brokerage — direct or fractional securities access through a brokerage structure.
  • GIFT City — India-based international financial infrastructure may provide another route to overseas markets.
  • Indian funds and ETFs — some products give overseas market exposure without directly holding individual US shares.
  • Tokenised exposure — blockchain products may track stocks or funds but may not provide the same legal rights as traditional securities ownership.

This is one of the most important distinctions in global investing.

Costs Indian investors should check

  • INR-to-USD conversion;
  • remittance fees;
  • brokerage;
  • custody fees;
  • withdrawal fees;
  • ETF expense ratio;
  • tax on dividends or gains, where applicable.

Do not compare platforms only by “zero brokerage”.

Fractional shares

Fractional investing allows users to gain exposure to expensive US shares using smaller amounts. But investors should check who holds the underlying shares, whether fractions can be transferred, how dividends are handled, and what happens if the broker changes product structure.

LRS and cross-border investing

The Liberalised Remittance Scheme is relevant to many overseas investment routes for Indian residents. Rules and limits can change, so current RBI and provider guidance should be checked before remitting funds.

Traditional shares vs tokenised stocks

A token can track the price of a stock without automatically giving the same shareholder rights. Compare ownership, voting, dividends, custody, corporate actions, transferability and counterparty risk.

Related: tokenised stocks.

What happens if a platform fails?

Ask where the asset is legally held, whether client assets are segregated, who the custodian is, whether you can transfer the position, and what jurisdiction governs the account. This question matters as much as fees.

Frequently asked questions

Can Indian residents invest in US stocks?

Several access routes exist, subject to platform eligibility and applicable regulations.

Do I need a demat account?

It depends on the route. Traditional Indian demat accounts and overseas securities custody are not always the same structure.

Can Indians buy fractional US shares?

Some platforms offer fractional access, subject to provider structure and eligibility.

Is a tokenised stock the same as a US share?

Not necessarily. The legal rights can be very different.

What is LRS?

LRS is the RBI framework used for certain permitted remittances by resident individuals.
