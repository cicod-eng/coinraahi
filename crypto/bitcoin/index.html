Bitcoin Explained for India

Last updated: 6 September 2026

Bitcoin is often described as “digital gold”, “digital money” or an investment. Each phrase explains only part of it.

A better starting point is: Bitcoin is a blockchain-based asset whose ownership and transfers are recorded by a decentralised network rather than one company or bank.

Bitcoin is not a company

There is no Bitcoin CEO issuing shares. Owning BTC does not mean owning equity in a business. This separates Bitcoin from stocks, ETFs and corporate bonds.

Where does Bitcoin come from?

New BTC enters circulation through mining. Miners compete to add valid blocks to the network and receive protocol-defined rewards plus transaction fees. The issuance schedule is limited by Bitcoin’s protocol.

Why does Bitcoin have a supply limit?

Bitcoin’s protocol is designed around a maximum supply of 21 million BTC. That does not guarantee price appreciation. Scarcity is one property; market demand is another.

How does a Bitcoin transaction work?

  1. A wallet creates a transaction.
  2. The transaction is signed.
  3. It is broadcast to the Bitcoin network.
  4. Miners include it in a block.
  5. Additional blocks increase confirmation depth.

What is a Bitcoin wallet?

A wallet does not contain physical Bitcoin. It manages the keys needed to control Bitcoin recorded on the blockchain.

Exchange Bitcoin vs self-custody Bitcoin

If BTC remains on an exchange, the platform normally manages custody. If you withdraw BTC to a self-custody wallet, you control the keys. The market exposure may be similar, but operational responsibility is different.

Bitcoin fees

Bitcoin transaction fees depend on network demand and transaction characteristics. An exchange withdrawal fee may be different from the exact network fee paid by the exchange.

What can go wrong?

Bitcoin-specific risks include price volatility, exchange failure, key loss, phishing, wrong-address transfers and poor backups. Bitcoin does not eliminate the need for operational security.

Bitcoin and India

Indian users should also consider platform KYC, INR access, VDA tax, transaction records and withdrawal support. Use the tax pillar rather than relying on old social-media summaries.

Common misconceptions

“Bitcoin is anonymous.” Bitcoin transactions are publicly visible, although addresses are pseudonymous.

“Bitcoin can be reversed by support.” Confirmed transactions generally cannot be reversed by a central administrator.

“One BTC is too expensive, so I cannot buy Bitcoin.” BTC is divisible; platform minimums vary.

Frequently asked questions

Is Bitcoin legal in India?

Bitcoin is used within India's broader VDA tax/compliance environment, but it is not legal tender.

Do I need to buy one whole Bitcoin?

No. Bitcoin is divisible.

Is Bitcoin safer than stablecoins?

They have different risk structures. Bitcoin has no central stablecoin issuer, but its price is far more volatile.

Where should I keep Bitcoin?

That depends on your custody needs, experience and balance size.
