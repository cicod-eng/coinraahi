Stablecoins Explained for India

Last updated: 6 September 2026

Stablecoins are designed to track the value of assets such as the US dollar, but they are not the same as a bank deposit. USDT and USDC can exist on multiple blockchain networks, can be held on exchanges or wallets, and carry issuer, custody and network risks. For users in India, there are also practical questions around INR access, tax records and moving funds between platforms.

What is a stablecoin?

Stablecoins try to maintain a stable value relative to a reference asset, most commonly the US dollar. They do this through different reserve and issuance designs, and they are issued by private companies rather than governments. Because the peg is not guaranteed, a stablecoin can still lose value. Tether (USDT) and Circle (USDC) are two of the largest issuers.

USDT vs USDC

The two differ by issuer, reserve disclosures, network support, freeze controls, exchange availability and transparency. Neither is a bank deposit, and both carry issuer risk. See the full USDT vs USDC comparison.

How Indian users access stablecoins

Common routes can include buying on an INR-supported exchange, P2P where available, third-party fiat on-ramps, or receiving stablecoins from another wallet.

Same USDT, different networks

USDT can exist on multiple networks, including Ethereum, Tron, BNB Smart Chain and other supported chains. A USDT balance on one network is not automatically interchangeable with the same token on another network during a transfer. Before sending, confirm that both sender and receiver support the same network. Compare USDT on TRC20, ERC20 and BEP20.

Stablecoin transfer checklist

  • Check the token.
  • Check the network.
  • Verify the address.
  • Check memo or tag requirements.
  • Confirm the minimum deposit.
  • Check the withdrawal fee.
  • Consider a small test transfer first.

Where can you hold stablecoins?

  • Exchange — convenient but custodial.
  • Self-custody wallet — you control the keys, but you are responsible for backup and security.
  • Hardware wallet — can reduce online exposure but introduces device and backup responsibility.

Stablecoin risks

RiskWhat it means
Issuer riskDependence on the company behind the token
Reserve riskWhether backing assets are sufficient

Freeze riskSome issuers can freeze addresses

Network riskWrong network can cause transfer problems

Stablecoins and tax records in India

Stablecoins may still fall within India’s VDA framework depending on the transaction and facts. Keep transaction records and review current official tax guidance. See Crypto Tax in India.

Frequently asked questions

Is USDT the same as USD?

No. USDT is a crypto token designed to track the US dollar.

Is USDT safe?

It carries different risks from cash or bank deposits, including issuer and platform risk.

Which USDT network is cheapest?

Network fees can vary over time. Cost should not be the only consideration; compatibility matters.

Can USDT be frozen?

Some centralised stablecoin issuers have mechanisms that can restrict certain addresses.

Is USDC safer than USDT?

The two have different issuers, reserves and risk structures. “Safer” depends on what risk you are comparing.
