Why USDT costs more than the dollar rate in India

When more people want to move into USDT with rupees than out of it, the rupee price of USDT is bid up above the official USD/INR rate. Demand spikes when people use USDT as a way to hold dollars, or to move value between platforms and wallets, and supply on the INR side does not always keep up. The premium is a measure of that local demand, not a flaw in USDT itself.

For a fuller explanation, see why USDT is more expensive than the dollar in India.

How we measure it

  • The reference is a published USD/INR foreign-exchange rate, not an exchange's own quote.
  • The exchange price is the last USDT/INR trade on a public Indian exchange ticker.
  • The premium is the percentage difference between the two at the same point in time.

Frequently asked questions

Is the premium always positive?

No. It usually is in India because of local demand, but it can shrink to near zero or briefly turn negative when demand falls or liquidity is high.

Does this mean my exchange is overcharging me?

Not necessarily. A premium reflects market demand across the INR market, not a single platform's fee. Still, comparing your platform's price against this reference helps you see how much of what you pay is market premium versus platform spread or fee.

Is this data live?

No. The table is updated periodically and shows an “as of” date. The calculator uses the reference rate and the price you enter, so it reflects your input at that moment.
