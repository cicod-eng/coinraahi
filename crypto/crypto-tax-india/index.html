Crypto Tax in India: 30% Tax, 1% TDS & What Beginners Need to Know

Last updated: 6 September 2026 · Rules reflect FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27

India taxes crypto gains under a special regime, and it is simpler — and harsher — than most people expect. Every time you sell, swap or otherwise transfer a virtual digital asset (VDA), the gain is taxed at a flat 30% (plus cess, so effectively about 31.2%). A 1% TDS is also collected on qualifying transfers. You cannot offset losses, and you can deduct only your acquisition cost — not trading or network fees.

The short version

  • 30% tax (Section 115BBH) on every VDA gain, plus 4% cess.
  • 1% TDS (Section 194S) on qualifying transfers — a prepayment, not an extra tax.
  • No loss set-off and no carry-forward, even within crypto.
  • Only the cost of acquisition is deductible — fees are not.
  • Crypto-to-crypto swaps are taxable events.
  • Report every transfer on Schedule VDA in your ITR.

This is educational, not tax advice. The rules depend on your circumstances and can change. Use this to understand the framework, then confirm your own numbers and filing obligations with a qualified professional.

The 30% tax: Section 115BBH

Income from transferring a virtual digital asset — cryptocurrencies, NFTs and similar tokens — is taxed at a flat 30%, regardless of how long you held it or which income slab you fall into. There is no short-term/long-term distinction and no concessional rate for holding longer.

On top of the 30% sits the 4% health and education cess, which makes the effective rate about 31.2% before any surcharge that may apply at higher income levels.

Two rules make this regime unusually strict:

  • Only your acquisition cost is deductible. You can subtract what you paid to buy the asset, but not trading fees, gas fees, withdrawal charges or anything else.
  • Losses cannot be offset or carried forward. If one VDA drops and another rises, you cannot net them. If your whole year is a loss, you cannot use that loss against other income, and you cannot carry it into the next year.

The 1% TDS: Section 194S

On qualifying VDA transfers, a 1% TDS is collected on the consideration. The key thing to understand is that TDS is a prepayment of your tax, not an additional tax. It is credited against what you owe and can be refunded if it exceeds your final liability. You will see it in Form 26AS and your Annual Information Statement (AIS).

Who deducts it depends on where you trade:

  • On an Indian exchange, the exchange generally deducts TDS automatically.
  • On peer-to-peer trades, the buyer is typically responsible for deducting it.
  • On Binance, which may not automatically deduct TDS for Indian residents in the same way a domestic exchange does, the responsibility can fall on you — a point many people miss until filing time.

TDS applies once your aggregate transfers cross a threshold in a financial year. The threshold is ₹50,000 for a "specified person" — generally an individual or HUF without income from business or profession, or whose business/profession turnover in the previous year was below the statutory limit — and ₹10,000 for other payers. Confirm which applies to you rather than assuming.

What counts as a taxable event

The most important correction most beginners need: it is not only "cashing out to INR" that is taxable. Transfers include:

  • Selling crypto for rupees.
  • Crypto-to-crypto swaps — trading BTC for USDT is a taxable transfer, even though no rupees are involved.
  • Using crypto to pay for goods or services.

A few things are taxed differently:

  • Airdrops, mining and staking rewards are generally taxed at your normal slab rate on the fair market value when you receive them; if you later sell, the gain on top is taxed at 30%.
  • Gifts of crypto above ₹50,000 in a year can be taxable in the recipient's hands under Section 56(2)(x), with exemptions for specified relatives.

How to work out what you owe

Here is the framework in steps, with a worked example.

  1. For each transfer, take the sale consideration and subtract the acquisition cost. That is your gain on that transfer.
  2. Add up only your gains. Because losses cannot be offset, you ignore losing transfers — you do not net them against gains.
  3. Apply 30% to the total gain, then add the 4% cess.
  4. Subtract the 1% TDS already collected against your account.
  5. If the remaining amount is large, check whether advance tax is due during the year.

Worked example. You buy USDT worth ₹1,00,000 and later sell it for ₹1,50,000. Your gain is ₹50,000. Tax is 30% of ₹50,000 = ₹15,000, plus 4% cess = ₹600, so ₹15,600 in total. If 1% TDS of ₹1,500 was already collected on the transfer, you credit that against the ₹15,600 and pay the balance. The 30% applies to the gain, not the full ₹1,50,000.

Filing: Schedule VDA and deadlines

VDA transactions are reported on Schedule VDA in your income-tax return, transaction by transaction — date of acquisition, date of transfer, cost, consideration and the resulting income. Keep records of every buy, sell and swap, because reconstructing them later is the painful part.

Return-filing deadlines vary by taxpayer category and can be extended by the Income Tax Department. Check the current deadline for the relevant assessment year rather than relying on a fixed date.

Because 1% TDS often does not cover a full 30% liability, advance tax may be required during the year if your total tax due crosses ₹10,000. Leaving it to the return can mean interest or a penalty.

What changed in 2026

The Union Budget 2026 kept the core rules intact — the 30% rate, the no-set-off rule and the 1% TDS were not reduced, despite industry requests. What did change is enforcement:

  • New penalties from 1 April 2026 for reporting non-compliance — up to ₹200 per day for failing to furnish crypto-asset transaction statements, and ₹50,000 for inaccurate statements.
  • The new Income Tax Act framework applies from Tax Year 2026-27. Returns for AY 2026-27 — which relate to income earned in FY 2025-26 — continue to be filed under the earlier Income-tax Act framework, with the VDA provisions preserved in substance under renumbered sections.
  • The VDA definition was clarified to explicitly include "crypto-asset", removing ambiguity about whether certain tokens fall within it.

Always verify current rules against the official Income Tax Department before filing, since tax law changes periodically.

Mistakes beginners make

  • Assuming losses cancel gains. They do not — this is the single most expensive misunderstanding under 115BBH.
  • Thinking the 1% TDS is the whole tax. TDS is a prepayment; 30% is the actual rate.
  • Ignoring crypto-to-crypto swaps. Swapping tokens is a taxable transfer even with no INR involved.
  • Deducting trading and network fees. Only acquisition cost is deductible — fees are not.
  • Skipping advance tax. If your TDS does not cover the liability, waiting until July can cost you interest.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 30% tax on my profit or the full amount?

On the gain, not the full sale amount. You subtract your acquisition cost from the sale consideration and the 30% applies to the difference.

Can I offset a losing trade against a winning one?

No. Losses from VDAs cannot be set off against other VDA gains or any other income, and cannot be carried forward to future years.

Do I pay tax if I just hold crypto and never sell?

Merely holding generally does not trigger the 30% transfer tax. But if you receive airdrops, mining or staking rewards, the receipt itself can be taxable at your slab rate, and a later sale of those rewards is taxed on the gain.

What if my 1% TDS is more than my tax due?

The TDS is credited against your liability and can be refunded if it exceeds what you owe. It shows up in Form 26AS and AIS.

Where do I report crypto transactions?

On Schedule VDA of your ITR, transaction by transaction. Keep your records from the start — reconstructing them at filing time is where most people struggle.
