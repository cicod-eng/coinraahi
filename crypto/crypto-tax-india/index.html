Crypto Tax in India 2026: 30% Tax, 1% TDS & What Actually Triggers Tax

Last reviewed: September 2026

Most explanations of Indian crypto tax start with two numbers — 30% tax and 1% TDS. Both numbers matter, but they answer completely different questions. The 30% rule concerns taxation of income arising from transfers of Virtual Digital Assets. The 1% rule concerns tax deduction at source on qualifying consideration paid for a VDA transfer. Confusing the two creates most beginner misunderstandings.

There is also an important 2026 change: India’s Income Tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026 for Tax Year 2026-27 onwards. So a correct 2026 guide needs to distinguish old Assessment Year filings from transactions occurring under the new Tax Year framework. The Income Tax Department explicitly confirms this transition. (Income Tax Department)

This is educational, not tax advice. The rules depend on your circumstances and can change. Use this to understand the framework, then confirm your own numbers and filing obligations with a qualified professional.

First: what is a Virtual Digital Asset?

India’s official VDA framework includes crypto-assets and qualifying NFTs while excluding items such as Indian currency, CBDC, foreign currency and certain notified digital assets. The Income Tax Department’s current VDA guidance also reflects the expanded crypto-asset wording introduced from 1 April 2026. (Income Tax Department) That means assets such as BTC, ETH, USDT and USDC can fall within the VDA framework. A stablecoin does not automatically become ordinary foreign currency simply because it targets USD.

What changed on 1 April 2026?

This is the part many articles now get wrong. India’s Income Tax Act, 2025 introduced the concept of a Tax Year from 1 April 2026. The Income Tax Department explains that Tax Year 2026-27 corresponds to income earned during FY 2026-27, and the old distinction between “previous year” and “assessment year” is removed for income governed by the new Act. (Income Tax Department) But the old system has not vanished instantly.

AY 2026-27 and Tax Year 2026-27 are not the same thing

This is critical:

  • AY 2026-27 relates to income earned during FY 2025-26. The Income Tax Department says these returns and related proceedings continue under the Income-tax Act, 1961. (Income Tax Department)
  • Tax Year 2026-27 relates to income earned from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 and falls under the Income Tax Act, 2025.

So do not write “Income Tax Act 2025 applies to AY 2026-27” — that is incorrect.

Is crypto income still taxed at 30% in 2026?

Yes, the special 30% treatment for income from VDA transfers continues. Under the old Act, Section 115BBH imposes a 30% rate on income from transfers of VDAs and restricts deductions and loss treatment. (Income Tax Department) Under the Income Tax Act, 2025, the equivalent special-rate provision appears in Section 194, where Table Sl. No. 4 applies a 30% tax rate to income from the transfer of a VDA, subject to the same core restrictions on deductions and losses. (Income Tax Department) So the rule survived the legislative transition — the section number changed.

30% applies to income — not your entire sale value

Suppose you buy crypto for ₹1,00,000 and later dispose of it for ₹1,30,000. Ignoring other complications, the economic gain is ₹30,000. The special tax rate applies to the qualifying income from the transfer, not simply to the full ₹1,30,000 consideration. That distinction matters.

What can be deducted?

Under the special VDA rule, the Income Tax Department states that no deduction is generally allowed for expenditure other than cost of acquisition, if any. (Income Tax Department) That means users should be cautious about assuming they can deduct ordinary trading-related expenses the way a conventional business might. For individual circumstances, professional tax advice is appropriate.

Can crypto losses reduce crypto gains?

This is one of the harshest parts of the VDA framework. The official VDA rule states that a loss from a VDA transfer cannot be set off against other income and cannot be carried forward, and the new Act preserves that restriction. (Income Tax Department) Do not assume BTC gain ₹1 lakh minus ALT loss ₹1 lakh equals zero taxable VDA income — the special rule prevents ordinary loss netting in the way many users expect. This is why transaction-level records matter.

What is the 1% crypto TDS?

TDS answers a different question. Under the old Income-tax Act, Section 194S provides for 1% TDS on consideration paid to a resident for transfer of a VDA, subject to applicable conditions and thresholds. (Income Tax Department) Under the Income Tax Act, 2025, the corresponding provision sits in Section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 8(vi) and continues the 1% mechanism. (Income Tax Department) TDS is not the same as your final tax, an exchange commission, or a second 1% capital-gains tax — it is a tax-deduction mechanism.

Who gets the ₹50,000 threshold?

This should be written exactly, because oversimplified articles often get it wrong. Under the current framework, the ₹50,000 threshold applies to an individual or HUF who falls into the relevant “specified person” category. Under the legacy Section 194S framework, this includes an individual/HUF with no income under “profits and gains of business or profession”, or whose previous-year business turnover does not exceed ₹1 crore, or professional receipts do not exceed ₹50 lakh. Other payers generally have the ₹10,000 threshold. (Income Tax Department) The new Act’s Section 393 preserves substantially the same ₹50,000 / ₹10,000 structure for Tax Year 2026-27 onwards. (Income Tax Department) So avoid writing “everyone gets ₹50,000”, and avoid reducing it to “₹10,000 only if you are tax-audited” — neither is precise enough.

What do these thresholds actually mean?

They concern whether TDS must be deducted under that rule. They do not mean “crypto profit below ₹50,000 is tax-free”. The TDS threshold and the 30% VDA income-tax calculation are separate concepts. This is a very important distinction.

Example: ₹40,000 crypto sale

Suppose a qualifying individual falls within the ₹50,000 specified-person threshold. If aggregate qualifying consideration during the tax year remains within that threshold, the 1% TDS mechanism may not be triggered under that provision. But that does not automatically mean any resulting VDA income is exempt from income tax. Again: TDS threshold ≠ income-tax exemption.

Who is responsible for TDS in P2P?

The answer depends on the payment structure. The Income Tax Department explicitly says:

  • Direct buyer and seller know each other — the buyer deducts TDS.
  • Exchange pays the seller directly — the exchange is responsible.
  • Broker is involved — responsibility depends on the exchange/broker arrangement.
  • VDA-for-VDA transaction — both sides may have withholding obligations because both sides are transferring VDAs. (Income Tax Department)

This is why a statement such as “the P2P seller pays the 1% TDS” is incorrect. The seller may ultimately bear tax on income, but the withholding responsibility is a different question.

What form was used under the older 194S system?

For a specified person deducting under Section 194S, the Income Tax Department states that Form 26QE was used as the challan-cum-statement, with the tax deposited within 30 days from the end of the month in which the deduction occurred. (Income Tax Department) But this is another place where 2026 changes matter.

What changed under the Income-tax Rules, 2026?

Under the new Income Tax Act framework, the Income Tax Department has consolidated certain challan-cum-statement workflows into Form No. 141. Its current guidance says Form 141 includes Schedule D for TDS on transfer of Virtual Digital Assets under Section 393(1), Table 8(vi), and generally must be filed electronically within 30 days from the end of the month in which deduction is made. (Income Tax Department) This is exactly why a 2026 article should not keep telling every reader “just file Form 26QE” — that advice depends on which law/year the transaction falls under.

Buying crypto with INR

Buying a VDA with INR creates an acquisition. The purchase itself is not the same thing as earning transfer income from disposing of a VDA. But keep the acquisition date, INR amount, fees and asset quantity — you may need these to establish cost of acquisition later.

Selling crypto for INR

This is a clearer disposal event. You need to determine consideration received, cost of acquisition, resulting income or loss, applicable special tax treatment and TDS records. Do not rely solely on an exchange’s final “profit” number without reconciling your records.

Crypto-to-crypto swaps matter

Suppose you swap BTC → ETH. It can feel like you never “cashed out”, but one VDA has been transferred in exchange for another. The official TDS guidance specifically contemplates VDA-for-VDA exchanges and recognises that both sides of such a transaction can involve transfer obligations. (Income Tax Department) Therefore “no INR involved = no tax event” is not a safe rule.

USDT to USDC can matter too

Suppose 1,000 USDT → 1,000 USDC. The economic dollar exposure changed very little, but the assets are different — you disposed of one token and acquired another. Do not treat the transaction as invisible simply because both are stablecoins. Keep the date, quantity disposed, INR value/reference, acquisition cost and resulting token acquired.

Moving crypto between your own wallets

Now compare Binance wallet → your MetaMask, or Hardware Wallet A → Hardware Wallet B. If you remain the beneficial owner before and after, this is fundamentally different from selling or exchanging the VDA to another person. However, keep evidence showing the movement was an internal transfer: TXID, sending address, receiving address, exchange withdrawal history and wallet records. Without that evidence, a later review may show only that the asset left an exchange. Good records establish what actually happened.

Do network fees create a taxable sale?

Do not answer this with one blanket rule without examining the mechanism. A network fee can involve disposal of a crypto asset — for example, using ETH to pay Ethereum gas. The precise tax treatment can depend on how the transaction is characterised and recorded. For significant amounts or complex DeFi activity, use a tax professional rather than assuming every gas payment is automatically irrelevant.

What about staking rewards?

Staking introduces another layer. Questions include when the reward is treated as income, what becomes its acquisition cost, and what happens when the reward token is later transferred. The answer can depend on facts and current guidance. Do not mix receiving a token with later transferring that token — they can involve different tax questions.

What about airdrops?

Same principle. Receiving an airdropped token and later disposing of it are separate events that may require separate tax analysis. The cost-of-acquisition position can also matter later. For material amounts, seek professional advice.

What about NFTs?

Qualifying NFTs are included in India’s VDA framework. The Income Tax Department’s VDA guidance expressly includes notified NFTs within the definition. (Income Tax Department) So do not assume NFT sales fall outside the crypto rules simply because the token is non-fungible.

What about tokenised stocks or gold tokens?

This requires more care. A blockchain token linked to a share, gold, an ETF or another financial asset does not automatically inherit the tax treatment of the underlying asset. You must first ask what legal product do I actually own? A tokenised asset may constitute a VDA depending on its structure and facts. That is why we keep separate guides for traditional US securities, tokenised securities, digital gold, gold ETFs and tokenised gold. See Real Stocks vs Tokenised Stocks and Gold ETF vs Digital Gold vs PAXG/XAUT.

TDS is not your final tax bill

Suppose ₹1,000 is deducted as VDA TDS. That does not mean “my final crypto tax is ₹1,000”. TDS is reported against your tax account and forms part of the broader income-tax reconciliation process. Your final liability depends on the actual taxable income and applicable rules. This is why users should reconcile TDS data with their records rather than treating it as a platform fee.

Why TDS can still hurt active traders

Even though TDS is not a final extra trading fee, it affects cash flow. An active trader can experience repeated deductions from transaction consideration before final tax reconciliation — especially noticeable for high-turnover strategies. So there are two separate effects: the economic trading result and the cash temporarily withheld through TDS. Do not mix them.

Crypto loss example

Suppose Bitcoin has a cost of ₹1,00,000 and sells for ₹1,50,000 (gain ₹50,000), while another VDA has a cost of ₹1,00,000 and sells for ₹60,000 (loss ₹40,000). A conventional investor might expect ₹50,000 − ₹40,000 = ₹10,000 net gain. But the VDA special rule restricts set-off of losses. (Income Tax Department) This is why the actual tax result can be much less intuitive than normal portfolio accounting.

Keep transaction-level records

At minimum, keep date/time, asset, quantity, INR value, acquisition cost, sale/swap consideration, trading fee, TDS, exchange, wallet address where relevant, TXID and transfer purpose. Do not rely on “I know I was down overall this year” — the tax framework looks at transactions and specific income/loss rules, not just your emotional sense of whether the portfolio won or lost.

Crypto reporting is becoming more structured

The new Income Tax Act, 2025 includes Section 509, which creates an obligation for prescribed reporting entities to furnish information about crypto-asset transactions. (Income Tax Department) That matters strategically: the direction of regulation is toward more structured transaction reporting, not less. Users should therefore assume record quality will become more important over time.

What Section 509 does not mean

It does not mean every individual personally files a Section 509 report. The obligation applies to prescribed reporting entities. But it reinforces the need for users to keep records that can be reconciled with exchange/platform reporting.

The CoinRaahi crypto-tax framework

Instead of memorising two percentages, classify every transaction using five questions:

  1. What asset moved? BTC? ETH? USDT? NFT?
  2. Did beneficial ownership change? Was it a sale/swap — or just your own wallet transfer?
  3. What consideration was received? INR? USDT? ETH? Another VDA?
  4. What was the acquisition cost? Can you prove it?
  5. Who had the TDS responsibility? Buyer? Exchange? Broker? Another party?

This framework is much more useful than “crypto tax in India = 30% + 1%”.

Bottom line

Indian crypto tax in 2026 is not accurately summarised as “30% tax plus 1% TDS on everything”. A better summary is:

  • Income from qualifying VDA transfers remains subject to a special 30% tax rate.
  • Loss set-off and deductions are heavily restricted.
  • 1% TDS can apply to qualifying VDA transfer consideration, subject to payer category, threshold and transaction structure.
  • The person responsible for TDS is not always the seller or always the exchange.
  • A crypto-to-crypto trade can matter even if no INR is received.
  • A transfer between your own wallets is a different factual event from a sale.
  • From 1 April 2026, the Income Tax Act, 2025 and Tax Year framework apply to new-period income, while AY 2026-27 still belongs to the old Act framework. (Income Tax Department)

The strongest habit is not memorising a tax percentage. It is being able to reconstruct what you acquired → what you transferred → what you received → what it cost → who withheld tax for every material transaction.

Frequently asked questions

Is crypto taxed at 30% in India?

Income from qualifying VDA transfers is subject to a 30% special tax rate, with applicable surcharge and cess. The new Income Tax Act, 2025 preserves the 30% special-rate treatment under Section 194. (Income Tax Department)

Is there 1% TDS on every crypto transaction?

No. The VDA TDS rule has payer-category thresholds and depends on the transaction structure. (Income Tax Department)

What is the ₹50,000 TDS threshold?

It applies to the specified individual/HUF category defined in the law. Other payers generally use a ₹10,000 threshold. (Income Tax Department)

Does ₹50,000 mean crypto gains below that amount are tax-free?

No. The ₹50,000 figure is a TDS threshold, not a general VDA income-tax exemption.

Can I offset one crypto loss against another crypto gain?

The special VDA rule restricts loss set-off and prohibits carry forward of VDA losses. (Income Tax Department)

Is swapping BTC for USDT taxable?

It can involve a transfer of one VDA for another. The absence of INR does not automatically remove the transaction from the VDA framework.

Is moving crypto to my own wallet taxable?

A genuine movement between wallets you control is factually different from a sale or exchange, but maintain evidence of ownership and transaction history.

Does the Income Tax Act, 2025 apply to AY 2026-27?

No. The Income Tax Department states that AY 2026-27 relates to FY 2025-26 and continues under the Income-tax Act, 1961. The new Act applies from Tax Year 2026-27, beginning 1 April 2026. (Income Tax Department)
