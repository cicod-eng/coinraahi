Binance vs CoinDCX vs CoinSwitch: Which Features Matter in India?

Last updated: 6 September 2026 · Fees and features verified 6 September 2026

These three platforms are not really competitors in the way their marketing suggests — they solve different problems. The single biggest difference is how your rupees get in: CoinDCX and CoinSwitch take UPI and IMPS directly; Binance typically routes INR through P2P rather than a built-in INR wallet. Everything else — fees, TDS, and whether you can move your crypto off the platform — follows from that. If you are new and want the simplest INR on-ramp, an Indian platform may suit you better. If you want lower spot fees, more assets and the ability to withdraw to your own wallet, Binance may be the more relevant choice.

The short version

  • INR in: CoinDCX & CoinSwitch use direct UPI/IMPS; Binance typically routes INR through P2P.
  • Fees: Binance 0.10% spot (0.075% with BNB), publicly verifiable; CoinDCX is VIP-level dependent; CoinSwitch prices via spread plus service fees.
  • TDS: CoinDCX & CoinSwitch deduct it automatically; Binance may not handle it for you.
  • Withdrawals: Binance and CoinDCX let you withdraw crypto; CoinSwitch may be INR-only.
  • Decide by INR access first, then by whether you want to hold crypto off-platform.

INR access: the deciding factor

For most people in India, this single question matters more than any fee: can I deposit rupees directly?

  • CoinDCX and CoinSwitch accept UPI, IMPS, NEFT and RTGS deposits, usually free and instant. You complete KYC once and rupees move in the way you already pay for everything else.
  • Binance does not offer the same built-in INR wallet. INR typically moves through its P2P marketplace, where you buy USDT from another user and pay them by UPI or bank transfer. That can add a markup and a counterparty risk we detail in the Binance India guide.

If a direct UPI deposit matters to you, the answer is already CoinDCX or CoinSwitch, not Binance. The rest of this comparison is for people still deciding between the two Indian platforms — or who are willing to use P2P to reach Binance's advantages.

Fees compared: why a headline rate is not enough

CoinRaahi reviewed the public fee documentation for all three platforms in September 2026. The result is not a clean 0.1% vs 0.2% vs 0% ranking:

  • Binance publishes a standard Spot fee of 0.100% maker / 0.100% taker for a Regular User, dropping to 0.075% with the eligible BNB discount. This is the easiest of the three to verify publicly. (Binance)
  • CoinDCX sets Spot fees by VIP Level, so there is no single platform-wide rate you can quote without stating the tier. Its crypto withdrawal minimums and fees also vary by token. (CoinDCX)
  • CoinSwitch does not quote a simple maker/taker fee in the same way. Its API documentation states that fees can be user-specific, and its Terms allow a separate service fee on certain instant orders plus transaction fees on some fiat or crypto transfers and withdrawals. (CoinSwitch)

So a “cheapest exchange” ranking built only from a public fee table is unreliable. Binance’s 0.10% base fee is publicly verifiable, but CoinDCX and CoinSwitch do not give you one headline number that holds for every user — and the P2P markup on the way into Binance can still erase its headline advantage. The number that matters is the quote shown just before you confirm a trade. See our crypto exchange fees guide for the full public-data methodology.

TDS: automatic or your job

The 1% TDS under Section 194S applies whichever platform you use, but who handles it differs:

  • CoinDCX and CoinSwitch deduct it automatically on qualifying transfers. You still file your return, but the deduction and deposit are done for you.
  • Binance may not handle it for you. On P2P trades the responsibility to deduct, deposit and report can fall on you depending on the transaction — enough extra paperwork that it is a real consideration, not a footnote. Details in our crypto tax guide.

Withdrawals and what you actually own

This is where the platforms diverge in a way that is easy to miss until it matters.

  • Binance and CoinDCX let you withdraw crypto to an external wallet (Binance now collects Travel Rule details on every external transfer; CoinDCX charges a network fee).
  • CoinSwitch — check current asset and network availability before committing, as withdrawal support can vary by asset and account. If every exit has to be an INR sale, that also turns each withdrawal into a taxable event and means you never truly hold the asset yourself.

This is the kind of detail to confirm inside the app before you commit, because it changes what "owning" crypto means on that platform. If moving coins to your own wallet is part of your plan, verify the withdrawal option exists first — see our wallet basics for why self-custody matters.

Who each platform suits

Rather than end with "it depends", here is how the three tend to map to different needs:

  • CoinSwitch may be more relevant if you are a first-time buyer who wants the simplest possible app and INR in/out, and you do not need to withdraw crypto to your own wallet.
  • CoinDCX may be more relevant if you want direct INR deposits and real trading features, crypto withdrawals, and clearer fees — the middle option for people who trade more than occasionally.
  • Binance may be more relevant if you want the widest range of assets, low spot fees, and the ability to hold crypto off-platform — and you are comfortable with P2P and handling your own TDS.

The decision tree is simple: start with INR access, then ask whether you need to withdraw crypto. Answer those two and the platform almost picks itself.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, Binance, CoinDCX or CoinSwitch?

On the published base Spot fee, Binance (0.10%, or 0.075% with BNB) is the most clearly verifiable. CoinDCX prices by VIP Level and CoinSwitch by a mix of spread and service fees, so neither has one headline rate you can rank without more detail. Binance's P2P markup on the way in can still erase that headline advantage for INR buyers.

Can I deposit INR directly on Binance?

Binance typically routes INR through P2P rather than a built-in INR wallet; you buy from other users. CoinDCX and CoinSwitch take direct UPI/IMPS deposits.

Which platform deducts TDS automatically?

CoinDCX and CoinSwitch deduct 1% TDS automatically on qualifying transfers. Binance may not handle it for you, so the obligation can fall on you depending on the transaction.

Can I withdraw crypto from CoinSwitch?

Withdrawal support can vary by asset and account, so confirm current availability inside the app before committing. If crypto cannot be withdrawn to an external wallet, every exit becomes an INR sale, which changes whether you can hold the asset yourself.

Which is best for a beginner?

CoinSwitch is the simplest INR in/out experience, but its spread and possible withdrawal limits matter. If you want direct INR plus the option to withdraw crypto, CoinDCX is a better all-rounder. Binance is the choice once you are comfortable with P2P.
