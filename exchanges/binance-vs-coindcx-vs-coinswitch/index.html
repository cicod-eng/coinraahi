Binance vs CoinDCX vs CoinSwitch: Which Features Matter in India?

Last updated: 6 September 2026 · Fees and features verified 6 September 2026

These three platforms are not really competitors in the way their marketing suggests — they solve different problems. The single biggest difference is how your rupees get in: CoinDCX and CoinSwitch take UPI and IMPS directly; Binance makes you buy through P2P. Everything else — fees, TDS, and whether you can move your crypto off the platform — follows from that. If you are new and want the simplest INR on-ramp, an Indian platform wins. If you want lower spot fees, more assets and the ability to withdraw to your own wallet, Binance is the stronger choice.

The short version

  • INR in: CoinDCX & CoinSwitch use direct UPI/IMPS; Binance is P2P only.
  • Fees: Binance 0.10% spot (0.075% with BNB); CoinDCX ~0.2%; CoinSwitch via spread (~0.5–2%).
  • TDS: CoinDCX & CoinSwitch deduct it automatically; Binance leaves it to you.
  • Withdrawals: Binance and CoinDCX let you withdraw crypto; CoinSwitch may be INR-only.
  • Choose by INR access, then by whether you want to hold crypto off-platform.

INR access: the deciding factor

For most people in India, this single question matters more than any fee: can I deposit rupees directly?

  • CoinDCX and CoinSwitch accept UPI, IMPS, NEFT and RTGS deposits, usually free and instant. You complete KYC once and rupees move in the way you already pay for everything else.
  • Binance has no direct INR deposit. You buy USDT through its P2P marketplace from another user, paying them by UPI or bank transfer. That adds a 1–3% markup and a counterparty risk we detail in the Binance India guide.

If a direct UPI deposit matters to you, the answer is already CoinDCX or CoinSwitch, not Binance. The rest of this comparison is for people still deciding between the two Indian platforms — or who are willing to use P2P to reach Binance's advantages.

Fees compared

Fee structures differ more than the headline numbers suggest:

  • Binance charges a transparent 0.10% maker/taker on spot, dropping to 0.075% if you pay fees in BNB. It is the lowest of the three on headline rate — but remember the P2P markup on the way in.
  • CoinDCX sits around 0.2% for spot, falling to roughly 0.03–0.04% at high monthly volumes, with extra discounts for holding its native token.
  • CoinSwitch does not quote a maker/taker fee on its standard app — it prices through a spread, typically around 0.5–2% depending on the asset. That cost is baked into the buy/sell price you see, which makes it easy to overlook.

The honest takeaway: on pure spot fees, Binance is usually cheapest, CoinDCX is mid, and CoinSwitch's spread makes it the hardest to price and often the most expensive for frequent trading. Check the live figures on Binance, CoinDCX and CoinSwitch.

TDS: automatic or your job

The 1% TDS under Section 194S applies whichever platform you use, but who handles it differs:

  • CoinDCX and CoinSwitch deduct it automatically on qualifying transfers. You still file your return, but the deduction and deposit are done for you.
  • Binance does not deduct it for Indian residents. On P2P trades the responsibility to deduct, deposit and report can fall on you — enough extra paperwork that it is a real consideration, not a footnote. Details in our crypto tax guide.

Withdrawals and what you actually own

This is where the platforms diverge in a way that is easy to miss until it matters.

  • Binance and CoinDCX let you withdraw crypto to an external wallet (Binance now collects Travel Rule details on every external transfer; CoinDCX charges a network fee).
  • CoinSwitch — one source reports it does not support withdrawing crypto to an external wallet, even on its Pro product, meaning every exit has to be an INR sale. That turns every withdrawal into a taxable event and means you never truly hold the asset yourself.

This is the kind of detail to confirm inside the app before you commit, because it changes what "owning" crypto means on that platform. If moving coins to your own wallet is part of your plan, verify the withdrawal option exists first — see our wallet basics for why self-custody matters.

Who each platform suits

Here is where we are willing to take a position rather than end with "it depends":

  • Choose CoinSwitch if you are a first-time buyer who wants the simplest possible app and INR in/out, and you do not care about withdrawing crypto to your own wallet.
  • Choose CoinDCX if you want direct INR deposits and real trading features, crypto withdrawals, and transparent-ish fees — the middle option for people who trade more than occasionally.
  • Choose Binance if you want the widest range of assets, the lowest spot fees, and the ability to hold crypto off-platform — and you are comfortable with P2P and self-remitting TDS.

The decision tree is simple: start with INR access, then ask whether you need to withdraw crypto. Answer those two and the platform almost picks itself.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, Binance, CoinDCX or CoinSwitch?

On spot fees alone, Binance (0.10%, or 0.075% with BNB) is usually cheapest, CoinDCX (~0.2%) is mid, and CoinSwitch's spread (~0.5–2%) is often the most expensive. But Binance's P2P markup on the way in can erase that advantage for INR buyers.

Can I deposit INR directly on Binance?

No. Binance has no direct INR deposit; you buy through P2P from other users. CoinDCX and CoinSwitch take direct UPI/IMPS deposits.

Which platform deducts TDS automatically?

CoinDCX and CoinSwitch deduct 1% TDS automatically on qualifying transfers. Binance does not for Indian residents, so the obligation can fall on you.

Can I withdraw crypto from CoinSwitch?

One source reports CoinSwitch does not support crypto withdrawals to external wallets, making every exit an INR sale. Confirm this inside the app before committing, as it changes whether you can hold the asset yourself.

Which is best for a beginner?

CoinSwitch is the simplest INR in/out experience, but its spread and possible withdrawal limits matter. If you want direct INR plus the option to withdraw crypto, CoinDCX is a better all-rounder. Binance is the choice once you are comfortable with P2P.
