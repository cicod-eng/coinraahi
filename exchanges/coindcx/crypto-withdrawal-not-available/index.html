CoinDCX Crypto Withdrawal Not Available? Why It Happens and What You Can Do

By Arjun Mehta · Research Editor · How we review

Last updated: 23 September 2026 · CoinDCX supports crypto withdrawal to verified, compliant wallets, subject to compliance and security checks

A “crypto withdrawal not available” message on CoinDCX almost always comes down to one of three different situations, and they each have a different fix. It could be the INR withdrawal (money out to your bank account), the crypto withdrawal (a coin out to an external wallet), or a specific transfer sitting under review. CoinDCX does support crypto withdrawals — but only to wallets that pass its compliance and security checks — so the question is usually which withdrawal is blocked and why, not whether the feature exists at all. Work out which of the three you are looking at before you retry, message anyone, or assume your money is stuck.

The short version

  • CoinDCX has two different withdrawals: INR (to a bank account) and crypto (to an external wallet). They are gated by different rules.
  • INR withdrawal is normally available — but incomplete KYC, a pending bank-account verification, an amount below the ₹100 minimum, or a 24-hour security hold can block it.
  • Crypto withdrawal is supported, but only to wallets that pass CoinDCX’s compliance and security checks — and a specific transfer can still be held for review.
  • A coin being listed and tradeable does not always mean you can withdraw it to your own wallet — it depends on the asset, network and your account.
  • Check what your own account shows, and never pay anyone to “unlock” a withdrawal.

Two different withdrawals, two different sets of rules

Most of the confusion behind “why can’t I withdraw” starts here: INR and crypto are separate systems with separate blockers. Mixing them up sends you looking at the wrong fix.

INR withdrawal vs crypto withdrawal on CoinDCX
Withdrawal type What it moves Typical blockers
INR withdrawal INR balance out to your bank account Incomplete KYC, pending bank-account verification, below ₹100, a 24-hour security hold
Crypto withdrawal A coin or token to a self-custody wallet or another exchange Unverified wallet, a restricted or high-risk source, a transfer under review, an unsupported network or token

If you can sell a coin to INR and withdraw the INR, but cannot withdraw the coin itself, that is the pattern to recognise: the INR off-ramp is open, while the crypto path depends on wallet verification and compliance. Keeping these two apart is the difference between a quick fix and a long wait.

Why a CoinDCX crypto withdrawal may be unavailable

CoinDCX’s current position is that you can withdraw crypto to an external wallet — but only to a verified and compliant wallet or exchange, and only after the wallet clears CoinDCX’s compliance and security checks. Its help centre also blocks deposits from restricted or high-risk sources and says every wallet has to pass those checks before a transaction is processed. That is the real, current rule — not a flat ban.

This is a different picture from a few years ago. During 2023–2025, reporting described CoinDCX’s crypto withdrawals as restricted or rolled out on an opt-in, phased basis — The Hindu, for example, reported on the confusion that restriction caused. Treat that period as historical background: the current, official position is that crypto withdrawal exists and is gated by wallet verification and compliance rather than by a blanket block.

So when a withdrawal looks “not available” today, the cause is usually one of a few specific things rather than an account-wide ban: the destination wallet has not passed verification, the asset or network is not supported, the request is a flagged transfer under review, or — on the Web3 side — the product you are in simply does not offer outbound transfers at all (more on that below).

CoinDCX has more than one external-transfer flow

One reason people reach the wrong conclusion is that CoinDCX has several different features that can move assets out, and they are not the same thing. Keep these apart:

  • Regular crypto withdrawal — the standard flow in the official withdrawal guide: Portfolio → Coins → Withdraw → pick the token → say whether the receiving wallet is yours → network → address → amount → withdrawal password → OTP. This is the path most people mean, and it is available once the wallet clears verification.
  • Crypto Withdrawal (CW) Opt-In — a separately managed process with its own eligibility criteria. CoinDCX states that CW Opt-In and External Wallet Transfer are two separate features; having one does not give you the other.
  • External Wallet Transfer — a dedicated feature for eligible delisted tokens only, so you can move those specific tokens out. It “does not grant or modify your regular Crypto Withdrawal access.”
  • CoinDCX Web3 — a different product that, for FIU and AML compliance reasons, does not support transfers to external wallets or exchanges at all. If you are inside the Web3 product, that is a product limit, not an account-level ban on regular CoinDCX.

Because these are separate, two people can open the same app and see different options — which is why “it worked for my friend” is not a reliable guide.

Check this first, in order

Work down this list before doing anything else. Each step rules out a layer of the problem.

  1. Which withdrawal is this? INR to your bank, or crypto to an external wallet? They have different rules.
  2. Is the option missing, or is a specific transfer on hold? A missing option may be an account-, product-, asset- or network-level limitation; a held transfer is a transaction-level review. The fix is different.
  3. What does the on-screen message say? If CoinDCX shows a reason or a waiting period, that specific message matters more than any general guide.

If the INR withdrawal is blocked

CoinDCX’s official support article lists four reasons an INR withdrawal can fail, and they are all things you can check directly:

  • KYC not completed. Identity verification must be done before core account features are available.
  • Bank account verification pending. The linked bank account needs to be verified, not just added.
  • Below the minimum. The minimum INR withdrawal is ₹100.
  • A 24-hour cooling period is active. This kicks in after a recent security change — enabling or changing 2FA, changing your mobile number, email or registered bank account, a mobile-number swap, or logging in with a backup code.

If all four check out and the INR withdrawal still fails, contact CoinDCX support through the in-app chat — the official channel — with your account details and the error you saw.

If the crypto withdrawal is the problem

Here the answer splits into two cases, and it is worth separating them.

Case 1: The option is not there at all

Before you conclude anything, confirm what you are actually looking at. Check you are in the regular CoinDCX app and not CoinDCX Web3 (which has no outbound transfers), and that you have picked the specific asset and network you want — a token can be tradeable while its withdrawal is not offered for you. The receiving wallet also has to be verified: per CoinDCX’s wallet-linking guidance, a self-custody wallet may need proof of ownership, and a hosted or third-party wallet may need beneficiary or remitter details. If the option is genuinely missing after all that, raise it with CoinDCX’s official support — they can tell you whether it is a wallet-verification step, a delisted-token situation, or something else on your account.

Case 2: A transfer is on hold

If you were able to initiate a withdrawal and it is stuck, CoinDCX says withdrawal requests go through several checks: blockchain confirmations (whose network processing time varies), internal risk assessments, and compliance reviews under AML/CFT guidelines. Its timing article does not publish a fixed completion time — a transfer “may take longer” if it is flagged for additional review, in which case you may be asked for supporting documents. Track the status under Pending Transactions, and if it stays on hold, contact official support with the details that let them find it quickly: the asset and amount, the network, the destination address, the date and time in IST, and a screenshot of the status screen.

For the broader question of whether a withdrawal will even reach your wallet, run the checks in our crypto transfer checklist — network match, memo/tag, and the right address — before and after any transfer you do manage to make.

What you can actually do

Put the situation against the action, rather than searching for a single “unlock”.

Situation and the corresponding action
Your situation What to do
INR withdrawal blocked Complete KYC and bank-account verification, check the ₹100 minimum, wait out any 24-hour security hold, then retry
Crypto withdrawal option missing Confirm you are in the regular app (not Web3), check the specific asset and network, complete any wallet verification; then raise it with official support
Crypto transfer on hold over 24 hours Contact CoinDCX support with asset, amount, network, address, date/time and a screenshot
You need to exit now INR withdrawal is usually the more direct off-ramp; for crypto out, check whether another platform offers self-custody withdrawal for your account

If your goal is specifically to hold the crypto yourself — not just to cash out — then the withdrawal path is the whole point, and it is worth comparing platforms on that before you buy. Our Binance vs CoinDCX vs CoinSwitch comparison and the exchange vs self-custody wallet guide both work through the “can I actually move it out” question, which is exactly what this situation turns on.

What not to do

  • Do not pay anyone to “unlock” the withdrawal. No legitimate support asks for payment to process a withdrawal.
  • Do not message “CoinDCX support” on Telegram, X or WhatsApp. These are almost always scams fishing for your login or OTP. Use the in-app or official support channel only.
  • Never share your OTP, password, or seed phrase. Support will not ask for these.
  • Do not keep re-submitting a transfer that is under review. It does not speed anything up and can create duplicate entries to untangle.

The rule you can reuse: check the exit before you enter

On any platform — CoinDCX, CoinSwitch, Binance, or anywhere else — before you deposit INR or buy a coin you intend to hold in your own wallet, confirm four things: can this asset be withdrawn, to which networks, at what fee, and is the withdrawal currently enabled for my account? A coin being listed and tradeable is not the same as being withdrawable. If you plan to self-custody, ask the exit question first — it is much easier to change your plan before the money goes in than after.

Frequently asked questions

Why is crypto withdrawal not available on my CoinDCX account?

CoinDCX does support crypto withdrawal, but only to wallets that pass its compliance and security checks. If the option is missing, the usual causes are an unverified destination wallet, an unsupported asset or network, a flagged transfer under review, or being in CoinDCX Web3 (which has no outbound transfers). Check what your account shows and, if a specific transfer is on hold, contact CoinDCX’s official support.

Is the INR withdrawal blocked for the same reason as crypto withdrawal?

No. INR withdrawal is normally available and is blocked for different, well-documented reasons: incomplete KYC, a pending bank-account verification, an amount below the ₹100 minimum, or a 24-hour cooling period after a recent security change. The crypto withdrawal path is a separate matter, gated by wallet verification and compliance.

Has CoinDCX always supported crypto withdrawals?

No. During 2023–2025, reporting described CoinDCX’s crypto withdrawals as restricted or rolled out on an opt-in, phased basis. CoinDCX’s current help centre position is that you can withdraw crypto from any verified and compliant wallet or exchange, subject to compliance and security checks. Treat the 2023–2025 restriction as historical background, and the current status shown in your account as the source of truth.

Can I withdraw crypto from CoinDCX to my own wallet?

Yes, in principle, if your wallet is verified and compliant and the transfer clears CoinDCX’s checks. For a self-custody wallet you may need to provide proof of ownership; for a hosted or third-party wallet you may need to submit beneficiary or remitter details. Supported networks, minimum amounts and fees also vary by token, so confirm what your account actually shows before buying an asset you intend to self-custody.

Will completing KYC unlock crypto withdrawal on CoinDCX?

KYC is necessary but not sufficient. Completing identity verification removes one common blocker, but crypto withdrawal also depends on the destination wallet passing verification and on any compliance review for the specific transfer. Completing KYC does not, by itself, guarantee a particular withdrawal will go through.

Is my crypto safe on CoinDCX if I cannot withdraw it?

Crypto held on CoinDCX is custodial: you have an account balance while CoinDCX manages the underlying wallet infrastructure and private keys. That a balance is recorded is a different question from whether you can move it out to your own wallet. For anything you intend to hold yourself, check withdrawal availability before you buy, not after.

What is the fastest way to move money out of CoinDCX?

For most users the INR withdrawal is the more direct off-ramp, because it is normally available once KYC and bank-account verification are complete and any 24-hour security hold has passed. The crypto withdrawal route depends on wallet verification and compliance checks, so it is usually the slower path. If you specifically need crypto in your own wallet, check withdrawal availability and supported networks before you buy.
