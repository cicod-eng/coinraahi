Crypto Exchange Fees & Withdrawals Explained

Last updated: 6 September 2026

The cheapest-looking crypto exchange is not always the cheapest one to use.

A platform may advertise a low trading fee but have a wider spread. Another may offer convenient INR deposits but charge more to move crypto to an external wallet.

To understand the real cost, follow the full path of the money.

The CoinRaahi total cost model

For a typical transaction:

Total Cost = Entry Cost + Trading Cost + Spread + Withdrawal/Transfer Cost + Exit Cost

This is more useful than comparing one fee percentage.

Trading fees

Crypto exchanges may use maker fees, taker fees, flat trading fees or tiered fee schedules. Your fee can depend on order type, trading volume, product and account tier.

What is spread?

Spread is the difference between the price at which you can buy and sell an asset.

Example. If a platform quotes a buy price of ₹100 and a sell price of ₹98, the effective cost is not explained by a 0.1% trading fee alone.

This matters especially on instant-buy or simplified conversion interfaces.

INR deposit costs

Check the bank transfer fee, payment gateway fee, minimum amount, limits and deposit processing time. Many platforms advertise free deposits, but the payment route itself may have limits or costs.

INR withdrawal costs

Before selling crypto back to INR, check the withdrawal fee, minimum withdrawal, daily limits, bank processing time and linked account requirements. An exchange can be easy to fund but less convenient to exit.

Crypto withdrawal fees

Crypto withdrawal fees can be different from blockchain network fees. An exchange may set its own withdrawal charge. Always check the asset, network, minimum, exchange fee and estimated amount received.

Network costs

Blockchain costs vary by network and market conditions. For example, Ethereum can become more expensive during congestion, while lower-cost networks may be cheaper but only if the receiving platform supports them.

The cheapest network is useless if the destination does not support it.

Minimum withdrawal

Minimum withdrawal amounts can matter for small users. If you buy only a small amount of crypto, the platform’s withdrawal minimum may make it impractical to move externally. Check this before buying.

The ₹10,000 test

A useful way to compare exchanges is to test the same route.

₹10,000 INR → Buy USDT → Withdraw USDT to a wallet

Measure:

  • INR credited
  • USDT received after purchase
  • Trading cost
  • Spread
  • Withdrawal fee
  • Final USDT received in wallet

This gives a much better comparison than a fee table. CoinRaahi should use this framework in future exchange tests.

Why “0% fee” can still cost money

A platform can advertise zero brokerage, zero trading fee or a free deposit. But the user may still pay through spread, conversion, withdrawal, FX cost or network fee.

Always ask:

How much of the asset do I actually receive?

Withdrawals are more than fees

A withdrawal can fail or delay because of KYC status, a security hold, network maintenance, minimum amount, an unsupported address/network or account review.

So when comparing exchanges, check both withdrawal cost and withdrawal availability.

Exchange withdrawal vs self-custody

Leaving crypto on an exchange avoids transfer fees but means the platform remains the custodian. Withdrawing to a personal wallet gives the user control of the keys but creates network risk, backup responsibility and transaction risk.

There is no universal answer. It depends on the user’s needs and ability to manage self-custody.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest crypto exchange in India?

It depends on the complete transaction route. Compare spread, trading fee, withdrawals and INR costs.

Are crypto withdrawal fees the same as network fees?

Not necessarily. Exchanges can set their own withdrawal charges.

Why is my withdrawal fee high?

It can depend on the asset, network, platform policy and market conditions.

Is a 0% trading fee really free?

Not always. Spread and withdrawal costs can still apply.

Should I always choose the cheapest network?

No. The receiving platform or wallet must support the same network.
