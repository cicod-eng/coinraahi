Binance P2P Payment Sent but Crypto Not Released? What to Do First

Last updated: 13 September 2026 · Binance P2P appeal timelines below follow its published rules; always follow the countdown shown on your own order

Your money has left your bank account and the seller has not released the crypto. The single most important thing to understand is that P2P is not a withdrawal. If you paid the correct amount to the payment account shown on the active Binance order, the crypto you bought is normally still protected in escrow — not in the seller’s wallet — while the order or appeal is being resolved. The mistake most buyers make is treating three different events as if they were one: paid (your bank shows a debit), received (the seller’s bank actually shows the credit), and released (the seller taps the button). The release clock starts when you mark the order as paid inside Binance, not when your bank debits the money. Sort out which stage you are at first, and the fix usually becomes obvious.

The short version

  • Confirm you marked the order as “paid” — if you did not, the seller has no release obligation and the order may auto-cancel.
  • Check the payment method: UPI/IMPS are near-instant (seller should release within ~15 minutes); NEFT/other transfers can legitimately take longer.
  • Send your payment proof in the order chat, then wait out the countdown shown on the order screen.
  • If the seller still does not release, raise an appeal from inside the order — the crypto stays in escrow while support reviews it.
  • Do not cancel the order after paying, do not move the chat off-platform, and never fake a payment proof.

Understand what is actually holding the crypto

In a Binance P2P order, the seller’s crypto is moved into escrow the moment you are matched. Your INR goes directly from your bank account to the seller’s bank account — Binance never touches the fiat. Only the seller’s tap of Release moves the escrowed crypto into your Binance wallet. That one design choice explains everything else: if the seller does not release, the crypto does not disappear, and the dispute process exists precisely to force a decision on which of you gets it.

This also means the usual on-chain checks you would run for a stuck withdrawal do not apply. There is no transaction ID to look up on an explorer, because no blockchain transfer has happened yet. The evidence you need is your bank-side proof — the UPI reference or IMPS/NEFT reference and the bank statement entry — not a block hash.

Start here: three checks in order

Run these before you do anything else, because each one points to a different fix.

Where the release is stuck, and what each check tells you
Check What you are looking for If the answer is “no”
Did you mark the order as paid? The order status shows “paid” / “transferred” and the countdown is running Mark it now — the seller is not obliged to release before this, and the order may time out
Did the money actually reach the seller? Your bank statement shows a completed debit with the correct account and reference Check the account number and IFSC you paid to against the order details — a wrong account is a different problem
Has the release window passed? The on-screen countdown has ended and the seller still has not released Move to an appeal — do not keep waiting past the window

The first check is the one people skip. If you sent the money but never clicked “I have paid” / “Transferred” in the order, the seller literally has no signal that you paid and no obligation to release. Marking it late is almost always safe and is the fastest fix when a release looks “stuck”.

Case 1: the seller is just slow (or their bank is)

Most “not released” situations are this, and the seller is not necessarily scamming you. Message them in the order chat first, attach your payment proof, and give them a moment.

Real-time payments: UPI and IMPS

UPI and IMPS settle within seconds to a few minutes, so the seller should be able to see your money almost immediately. Binance’s published P2P rules expect a seller to release within about 15 minutes of a real-time payment. If the seller has not released after that window, you have a fair case to appeal rather than wait indefinitely.

Non-instant payments: NEFT and other bank transfers

NEFT is not an instant payment method. Although India’s NEFT system operates 24×7×365 in half-hourly settlement batches, the credit may still take longer than UPI or IMPS depending on the sending and receiving bank. Under Binance’s rules, a seller receiving a non-instant payment can be given up to two working days to confirm receipt before Binance treats the payment as received and can release the crypto manually. If the seller has not received the funds yet, follow the countdown and appeal instructions shown on your Binance order rather than assuming the seller is refusing to release.

Timelines here follow Binance’s published appeal rules as of September 2026. They are not a personal guarantee: the countdown on your own order is the number that actually applies, and a manual review can take longer.

Case 2: you have paid, but the seller will not release

If the seller is unresponsive, is claiming a payment that clearly went through never arrived, or is asking you to do something outside Binance, stop negotiating and open an appeal. Do it from inside the order so everything you send is attached to the order record that support will read.

The appeal option sits on the order page (it may only become active a short while after you mark the order as paid — often around 15 minutes). When you raise it, choose the reason that matches your situation, such as “I have paid but the seller has not released the crypto”, and attach the strongest evidence you have:

  • Transfer receipt showing the amount, the recipient account, and the time.
  • UPI / IMPS / NEFT reference number from your payment app or bank.
  • Order ID from the P2P order.
  • Chat screenshots showing you sent proof and the seller did not release.

What happens during the appeal

The appeal is not instant, but the design protects you while you wait: the seller’s crypto stays frozen in escrow for the whole dispute. When you appeal, the counterparty is generally given a short window — around 10 minutes — to respond. If they do not, the case moves to “Under Review” and Binance customer support takes over, which can contact the seller by email and mediate.

If support verifies that you paid the correct amount to the correct account, they can release the crypto from escrow to you manually — including in the common case where the seller simply went offline and never responded. This is the key reassurance: in a properly documented appeal, the escrow mechanism is designed to return the crypto to the buyer who actually paid.

Resolution speed varies. Straightforward cases with clean payment proof may be resolved quickly; cases where the seller disputes receipt or the payment is hard to trace can take longer, sometimes a couple of working days. Keep your proof organised so you can re-attach it if support asks.

The India-specific angles you should not confuse

Indian P2P users run into three ideas that get tangled up with “crypto not released”. They are related but different, and the fix for each is different.

A slow release is not the same as a frozen bank account

A bank-account freeze is when your bank places a lien on your account after a P2P or UPI inflow gets linked to a fraud complaint — the risk that has made Indian P2P sellers especially jumpy. That is a banking and cyber-cell matter, and it mostly hits the seller’s side, where incoming fiat can be traced back to a scam. It is not something a “release” button fixes. It is, however, one reason a seller may be slow or cautious: they are checking that your payment and the account name line up before they release. See our scam-prevention guide for how these freezes work and what records to keep.

Buyer protection is not the same thing as Shield Merchant compensation

Binance’s merchant-protection compensation for frozen bank accounts covers sellers whose receiving account gets frozen in a qualifying case — it is not a buyer-side guarantee that your purchase will always complete. As a buyer, your protection in a non-release dispute is the escrow and the appeal process, not a Shield claim. Do not assume a “verified merchant” badge removes all risk; it does not guarantee a clean source of fiat, only that the merchant has cleared certain requirements.

The account name matters more than most buyers realise

Before you pay, confirm the bank account name in the order matches the seller’s verified name. Paying a third party whose name does not match the order is how many disputes start, and it is also how your own account can end up in someone else’s fraud trail. Follow the payment instructions shown on the Binance order: if the merchant specifically asks you to leave the transfer remark blank or avoid crypto-related wording, follow the on-order instructions, but never invent a false payment purpose or misrepresent the transaction to your bank.

What not to do while you wait

  • Do not cancel the order after paying. Cancelling tells the system the deal never happened even though your money moved, which makes recovery harder.
  • Do not move the conversation to WhatsApp or Telegram. Support can only review what stays inside the order chat.
  • Do not fake or edit a payment screenshot. Altered proof is detectable and can get your own account restricted.
  • Do not pay anyone a “release fee”. Real support never asks for payment to process an appeal.
  • Do not share your OTP, password or seed phrase with the seller or anyone claiming to be support.

Bottom line: a rule you can reuse

Whenever a P2P release looks stuck, run the same three questions instead of panicking: Did I mark the order as paid? Did my money actually land in the seller’s account? Has the release window passed? If the answer to all three is yes, open the appeal from inside the order. If you paid the correct account, used an account in your own verified name, and kept valid payment proof, the crypto should remain protected in escrow while Binance reviews the dispute. The one thing that decides most of these cases is a clean, unedited payment proof tied to the order ID. Frequently asked questions

I paid the Binance P2P seller but they say they haven't received it. What do I do?

First confirm which payment method the order uses. UPI and IMPS are near-instant, so the seller should see the money within minutes; NEFT or other bank transfers can take longer to land. Send your payment proof in the order chat, then follow the release window for that payment method. If the seller still does not release, raise an appeal from inside the order with the transfer receipt.

How long does a Binance P2P seller have to release crypto in India?

It depends on the payment method. Binance's published P2P appeal rules give a seller about 15 minutes to release after a real-time payment such as UPI or IMPS. For non-instant payment methods, the seller may have up to two working days to confirm receipt before Binance steps in and can release the crypto manually. The on-screen countdown on your order is the figure that actually applies.

Can I cancel a Binance P2P order after I have already paid?

Do not cancel an order after paying. Cancelling tells the system the deal did not happen, even though your money has left your account, which makes recovery harder. If you have paid and the seller is not releasing, use the appeal process instead so the crypto in escrow can be reviewed and released to the correct side.

Is my crypto safe in a Binance P2P order while I wait?

In a P2P order, the crypto you are buying is held in Binance's escrow from the moment the order is matched, not in the seller's wallet. If you paid the correct amount to the account shown on the order and kept your payment proof, the crypto normally stays frozen in escrow while the dispute is reviewed, and Binance support can release it to you once your payment is verified.

Why did the seller ask me not to write 'USDT' or 'Binance' in the transfer note?

Some merchants ask for a blank remark or for you to avoid crypto-related wording in the transfer note. Follow the instructions shown on your order, but never invent a false payment purpose or misrepresent the transaction to your bank. Keep the payment itself to the merchant's own verified bank account shown on the order.

Does buying USDT on P2P trigger the 1% TDS or the 30% tax?

The two rules are different. India’s 30% VDA tax under Section 115BBH generally concerns income arising when a VDA is transferred; simply acquiring USDT does not by itself create a taxable gain for the buyer. However, Section 194S is different: when a buyer pays a resident seller for the transfer of a VDA, the buyer may have a 1% TDS obligation once the applicable annual threshold is crossed — generally ₹50,000 for a “specified person” and ₹10,000 for other payers. P2P and exchange-facilitated transactions can involve additional procedural rules, so check our India crypto tax guide or obtain professional tax advice for your specific transaction.

Is a P2P seller not releasing the same as a Binance withdrawal being pending?

No. They are different workflows. A P2P release issue is a dispute between you and a seller, settled through the order's appeal process with the crypto in escrow. A pending withdrawal is a transfer of crypto out of your account that can be stuck inside Binance, on the blockchain, or at the receiving side. The two have different checks and different support paths — see our guide to checking a pending withdrawal first.
