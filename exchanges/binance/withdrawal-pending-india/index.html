Binance Withdrawal Pending in India? Check TXID, Travel Rule and Network First

Last updated: 11 September 2026 · India's Travel Rule requirements on Binance have applied since 22 June 2026

A withdrawal stuck on “pending” is not the same as money that is gone. In almost every case the transfer is sitting at one of three layers — inside Binance, waiting on the blockchain, or uncredited at the receiving end — and the fix depends entirely on which layer it is in. India adds one more layer that many users do not yet know about: since 22 June 2026, Binance has applied India's Travel Rule requirements to every crypto deposit and withdrawal, and a missing or incorrect counterparty detail is now one of the first things Indian users should check when a withdrawal does not move. Before you panic, re-send, or message a “support” account on Telegram, run through the checks below in order.

The short version

  • Look for a transaction ID (TXID) first — it tells you which of the three layers the transfer is stuck at.
  • No TXID → the hold is inside Binance. Check for a Travel Rule prompt, a security-lock countdown, or a review notice.
  • TXID but “pending” on-chain → the network is still confirming. This is usually normal and resolves on its own.
  • TXID “confirmed” but nothing credited → the issue is the receiving side: wrong network, missing memo/tag, or not enough confirmations.
  • Do not re-send the same withdrawal, and never share your seed phrase or OTP with anyone claiming to be support.

The three-layer check: find where the transfer actually is

Everything else follows from one question: has Binance given you a transaction ID yet? That single detail sorts the problem into the correct layer, and each layer has a different owner and a different fix.

Where a pending withdrawal sits, and what each stage means
What you see Which layer What it means Who can fix it
No TXID at all Inside Binance Not yet broadcast on-chain; held for info, security or review Binance (and often you, by completing a prompt)
TXID, but explorer shows “pending / unconfirmed” Blockchain Broadcast, waiting for network confirmations No one — it resolves with the network
TXID, explorer shows “confirmed” Receiving side On-chain success; the receiving platform or wallet has not credited it The receiving platform / wallet (or a network/memo error on your side)

To check, open your withdrawal in Wallet → Transaction History, copy the transaction hash, and paste it into a block explorer for that network (for example, Tronscan for TRC20 or Etherscan for ERC20). What the explorer says decides your next step.

No TXID yet: the hold is inside Binance

If there is no transaction ID, the withdrawal has not yet been broadcast to the blockchain. The next step is to check Binance for an information request, a security restriction or an account review — rather than troubleshooting the destination wallet. In India right now, three causes dominate.

The Travel Rule prompt (the new one)

Since 22 June 2026, Binance has applied India's Travel Rule requirements to crypto transfers of any amount — so users should not assume that small withdrawals are exempt. On a withdrawal, Binance asks for the beneficiary's details:

  • Full name of the recipient
  • Country of residence
  • City / town / village
  • Name of the receiving exchange, if the crypto is going to another exchange

There is one useful shortcut: if you are sending crypto to yourself on another exchange, you normally only need to provide the name of the receiving exchange. If you are withdrawing to your own private wallet, Binance may ask you to confirm whether the wallet is private or hosted, and may offer a small “Satoshi test” transaction to verify ownership so you do not have to keep redeclaring the same wallet.

What matters for a pending withdrawal is the consequence. Binance's own guidance states that if the required information is missing or incorrect, the transaction may be delayed or not processed. In other words, a withdrawal that looks “stuck” is very often simply waiting for a counterparty detail you have not completed. Look for the pop-up or the field on the withdrawal screen, fill it accurately, and the transfer can proceed.

The automatic security lock

If you recently changed your password, reset or changed a two-factor authentication method, or completed another sensitive security operation, Binance may temporarily disable withdrawals. Depending on the security action, the restriction can last up to 24 to 48 hours. The withdrawal page will usually show the restriction and, where applicable, the remaining waiting period — so check the message shown in your account rather than relying on a generic timeframe.

The compliance or risk review

A withdrawal can also be held for a manual review if the account has triggered a risk flag. This is the category with no published timeline. Some users report the hold clearing in hours; more involved source-of-funds reviews can take much longer. If you see an appeal or “withdrawal appeal” option, submit what is asked — typically identity documents, a selfie, or evidence of the source of funds.

Timelines above are typical patterns reported by users, not guarantees. Binance does not publish a single fixed duration for reviews, so treat your on-screen message and the live status as the source of truth rather than any number in a guide.

TXID pending on the blockchain: usually just waiting

A transaction ID that shows “unconfirmed” on the explorer means Binance has done its part and the network is still processing. This is the least worrying layer, because it resolves on its own.

How long it takes depends on the network and how busy it is. Some networks such as TRON may confirm quickly under normal conditions, while Ethereum and other networks can take longer depending on congestion and the transaction conditions. During a busy period, even a normally fast network can slow down. If the explorer shows confirmations climbing, the transfer is progressing — leave it alone.

The one check worth doing here is the network. If you selected TRC20 but the destination only accepts ERC20, the transfer will never arrive regardless of how long you wait. That is not a “pending” problem — it is a wrong-network problem, and it moves you into the next section.

TXID confirmed but nothing credited: the receiving side

A confirmed transaction is a completed blockchain transfer. If the funds still do not show up, the problem has moved to the receiving platform or wallet — or to a detail you entered. Three checks, in order:

  • Network match. The sending and receiving networks must be the same. USDT sent on TRC20 will not appear in an ERC20 address.
  • Memo / tag / destination tag. Some networks and exchange deposits (notably XRP, XLM, and some EOS-based tokens) require a memo in addition to the address. If you omitted it, the receiving platform holds the funds but cannot match them to your account until you contact them.
  • Required confirmations. Exchanges often require more confirmations than the network's first one before they credit an account. “Confirmed” on the explorer does not always mean “credited” on the exchange yet.

This is the core distinction to internalise: on-chain success and account credit are two different steps. If the explorer says confirmed, stop looking at Binance — the ball is now with the receiving platform, and you should raise a ticket there with the TXID. Two India-specific confusions to rule out first

Indian users searching for “withdrawal pending” are sometimes describing a different problem than a Binance withdrawal at all. Ruling these out saves you from chasing the wrong support queue.

A Binance withdrawal is not the same as a P2P release

On Binance P2P, buyers and sellers settle directly. A seller who has received your payment but has not released the crypto is a P2P dispute, not a withdrawal stuck in a queue. The check is different: open the order, check its countdown, and if the release time has passed, raise a P2P appeal inside the order. This is a separate workflow from a crypto withdrawal.

A pending withdrawal is not the same as a frozen bank account

Indian users have also faced bank-account freezes or liens where P2P or UPI payments later became linked to cyber-fraud complaints. If your bank account — not your Binance balance — is blocked, that is a banking and cyber-cell matter, not something Binance can release. The signs are different: a bank-side freeze is a lien on your bank account, whereas a pending withdrawal is a status inside Binance. Keep the two separate so you raise the issue with the right party. This freeze risk is also part of why P2P pricing works the way it does — see our guide to buying USDT in India.

What to send Binance support

If you have completed any prompts, waited out any countdown, and the withdrawal is still stuck, contact support through the in-app or on-site support centre — the only official channel. Sending these six details in the first message usually gets the ticket moving faster than a vague “my withdrawal is pending”:

  • Transaction ID (TXID) — the hash from the withdrawal record.
  • Asset and amount — for example, 500 USDT.
  • Network — TRC20, ERC20, BEP20, or whichever you selected.
  • Destination address — and the memo or tag if the network uses one.
  • Date and time of the withdrawal (IST).
  • Screenshot of the withdrawal status page and any on-screen message.

What not to do while it is pending

  • Do not re-send the same withdrawal. It does not speed up the first one, and a second transfer can create a second problem to track.
  • Do not message “Binance support” on Telegram or X. Binance does not offer support through unsolicited DMs, and accounts claiming to be support there are almost always scams fishing for your details.
  • Never share your seed phrase, password, or OTP — not with support, not with anyone offering to “unblock” the transfer.
  • Do not pay anyone to “release” the funds. Real support does not ask for payment to process a withdrawal.

Bottom line: a rule you can reuse

The next time any crypto transfer looks stuck, run the same three questions instead of guessing: Is there a TXID? What does the explorer say? Is the receiving side expecting this network and memo? In India, add a fourth: did I complete the counterparty details the Travel Rule asks for? That sequence — platform, blockchain, receiving side, counterparty info — covers the overwhelming majority of “pending” situations, on Binance and on most other exchanges. Frequently asked questions

How long does a Binance withdrawal usually stay pending in India?

There is no single number. A withdrawal with a transaction ID waiting for blockchain confirmations may clear in minutes to a couple of hours. An automatic security lock after a password or 2FA change generally lifts within 24 to 48 hours. A compliance or Travel Rule review has no published timeline and can take longer. Check the transaction status and any on-screen message rather than assuming a fixed time.

Why does Binance India ask for the recipient's name and city before I can withdraw?

Since 22 June 2026, Binance has applied India's Travel Rule requirements to crypto deposits and withdrawals of every amount. The platform asks for the beneficiary's name, country of residence, city or town, and the receiving exchange if applicable, so the counterparty details can travel with the transaction. If the information is missing or incorrect, the withdrawal may be delayed or not processed.

My withdrawal shows pending but I have no TXID. Is my money safe?

A missing TXID usually means the withdrawal has not yet been broadcast to the blockchain. That does not by itself mean the funds are lost — the hold is inside the platform, most often a missing Travel Rule detail, a security lock, or a review. Check for an on-screen prompt or a countdown timer and complete any missing information.

Binance says the crypto was sent, but my wallet shows nothing. What now?

Check the transaction ID on a blockchain explorer. If it shows confirmed but the wallet still shows nothing, the likely issues are a wrong network, a missing memo or tag, or the receiving platform requiring more confirmations than the network has produced. Confirmed on-chain is not the same as credited to an account.

Is a pending withdrawal related to the 1% TDS or the 30% tax?

No. TDS and capital gains tax affect the tax treatment of certain transfers, not whether Binance releases a withdrawal. A pending withdrawal is a processing or compliance matter; tax applies separately, based on the transaction. For how the two connect, see our crypto tax guide for India.

Can I cancel a pending Binance withdrawal?

It depends on the stage. If no transaction ID exists and the withdrawal is still under Binance's control, the withdrawal page may offer a cancel option. Once a transaction ID has been generated and broadcast, the transfer cannot generally be cancelled from Binance's side — the funds are on the blockchain.

Will a Telegram Binance support account speed up my withdrawal?

No. Binance does not provide support through Telegram or unsolicited direct messages, and accounts claiming to be Binance support there are typically scams. They may ask for your seed phrase, password or OTP — never share these. Only use the official support channel inside the Binance app or website. For more on spotting these, see our scam prevention guide.
