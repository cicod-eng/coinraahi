Binance India Guide: KYC, INR, Fees, Withdrawals & Tax

Last updated: 6 September 2026 · Fees and rules verified 6 September 2026

Binance is available to users in India and is registered with FIU-IND — but it works differently from home-grown exchanges in one way that shapes everything else: you cannot deposit rupees directly. INR moves in and out through peer-to-peer (P2P) trading, not a built-in UPI wallet. Understand that one difference and the rest of Binance falls into place.

The short version

  • Availability: registered with FIU-IND (2024); registration is a reporting obligation, not a government licence.
  • INR: P2P only — buy USDT from other users via UPI, IMPS or bank transfer.
  • KYC: PAN or Aadhaar, a live selfie and a bank account verified by "penny drop".
  • Fees: 0.10% spot trading, or 0.075% if you pay fees in BNB.
  • Tax: 30% on gains plus 1% TDS — Binance does not deduct it for you.

Is Binance available in India?

Yes, with a qualification. Binance is registered with the Financial Intelligence Unit — India (FIU-IND) as a reporting entity under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA). It completed that registration in August 2024 and paid a penalty of roughly ₹18.82 crore to restore access, after being blocked for much of 2024.

What registration does and does not mean:

  • It does mean Binance must follow India's anti-money-laundering and KYC rules, and report to FIU-IND.
  • It does not mean Binance is licensed by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) or the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI).

In plain terms: FIU-IND registration is a compliance obligation, not a government endorsement. If this matters for your decision, confirm the current status on the FIU-IND website rather than relying on any secondary source, including this page.

How you move INR in and out: P2P, not UPI

The biggest practical difference between Binance and an Indian exchange like CoinDCX is that Binance does not offer a built-in INR wallet with direct UPI, IMPS or NEFT deposits. Instead, rupees move through Binance's peer-to-peer marketplace:

  1. You place an order to buy a stablecoin such as USDT from another user in India.
  2. You pay that seller directly by UPI, IMPS or bank transfer.
  3. Binance holds the crypto in escrow and releases it once the seller confirms payment.

Selling works in reverse: you transfer USDT to a buyer and receive rupees into your bank account. Before using P2P, you verify your bank account through a small "penny drop" — Binance deposits a tiny amount that you confirm.

Account-freeze risk is real. On P2P you deal with individuals, not Binance, for the rupee side of the trade. If money a seller sends you is later linked to fraud, your bank account can be frozen while it is investigated — a known problem in India that Binance does not resolve quickly. Trade only with well-rated counterparties and keep records of every transaction.

KYC: what you need

Binance requires full identity verification before you can trade or withdraw. For users in India that typically means:

  • PAN or Aadhaar — PAN is the usual choice because it ties into tax reporting.
  • A live selfie to confirm the document belongs to you.
  • A linked bank account verified by penny drop.

KYC is usually reviewed within minutes to a few hours, though it can take longer during busy periods. Until it is approved, withdrawal limits stay restricted. See our KYC & Account guide for the requirements shared across platforms.

Fees: spot, the BNB discount and withdrawals

Spot trading on Binance costs 0.10% for both makers and takers. If you pay fees using BNB — Binance's own token — you get a 25% discount, which brings the effective fee to 0.075%.

Withdrawing crypto: the 2026 Travel Rule change

From 22 June 2026, Binance tightened the data it collects from Indian users whenever crypto enters or leaves the platform. This follows India's adoption of the FATF Travel Rule under PMLA and FIU-IND oversight.

What changed:

  • Sending crypto out: you provide the recipient's full name, country, city/town and, where relevant, the receiving platform's name.
  • Receiving crypto: you provide the sender's full name, PAN or national ID, full address including state, city and PIN code, and country.
  • These details apply regardless of amount — there is no small-transfer exemption.
  • Trading within Binance is unchanged; the rules apply to external deposits and withdrawals.

This is not a Binance-only quirk — it reflects how India is applying the Travel Rule to regulated platforms. Expect a little more friction on deposits and withdrawals than in earlier years, and keep your recipient or sender details ready before you start a transfer.

Tax in India: 30% and 1% TDS — your responsibility

India taxes gains from virtual digital assets (VDAs) under a special regime:

  • 30% tax on gains under Section 115BBH, with loss-offset rules that differ from ordinary income.
  • 1% TDS on qualifying transfers under Section 194S, subject to thresholds and rules.

The practical point for Binance: it does not automatically deduct the 1% TDS for Indian residents the way several Indian exchanges do. On P2P trades, responsibility for deducting and depositing TDS can fall on the seller, who may need to file Form 26QE and report the transaction. Confirm your own obligations with a tax professional — rules depend on your circumstances and can change. Our crypto tax guide walks through the full detail.

Who is Binance right for?

The honest answer is that it depends on how you move INR and how much simplicity you value.

Binance tends to suit you if:

  • You want a wider range of coins and deeper trading features than most Indian platforms offer.
  • You are comfortable buying and selling through P2P rather than a direct UPI deposit.
  • You are prepared to handle 1% TDS and your own tax reporting.

An Indian exchange may suit you better if:

  • You want the simplest INR on-ramp with direct UPI.
  • You would rather the platform deduct TDS automatically.
  • You are new and want to avoid P2P counterparty and account-freeze risk.

Neither is universally better — the decision turns on how you fund your account and how much compliance work you want to take on. We compare the three main options side by side in Binance vs CoinDCX vs CoinSwitch.

Frequently asked questions

Is Binance legal in India?

Binance is registered with FIU-IND as a PMLA reporting entity, which makes it a regulated platform for anti-money-laundering purposes. That registration is not a licence from the RBI or SEBI and should not be read as a government endorsement.

Can I deposit INR directly on Binance?

Not through a built-in UPI/IMPS wallet. INR moves through the P2P marketplace, where you pay another user directly and receive crypto from escrow. Some sources claim direct UPI deposits exist, but that conflicts with how Binance's INR on-ramp actually works for most users — check the deposit options inside your own account to be sure.

Does Binance deduct 1% TDS automatically?

Generally no, for Indian residents. Several Indian exchanges deduct TDS at source; on Binance, and especially on P2P trades, deducting and depositing TDS can fall on you. Confirm your specific obligations with a tax professional.

Do I need a PAN to use Binance in India?

Yes, KYC requires a government ID — PAN or Aadhaar — plus a live selfie and a bank account you verify by penny drop. PAN is the usual choice because it ties directly into tax reporting.

Is my money safe on Binance?

Binance is a major exchange with FIU-IND registration, but holding funds there still means a third party controls them, and there is no government deposit insurance. Add P2P counterparty and account-freeze risk on the INR side, and the sensible approach is to keep only what you need on the exchange and take basic account security steps.
