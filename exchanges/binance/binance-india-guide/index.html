Binance India Guide 2026: KYC, INR Access, Fees, Withdrawals & Tax

Last reviewed: September 2026

Binance is available to eligible users in India, but using it is not as simple as asking “Is Binance legal in India?”. There are several separate questions: Is Binance currently accessible in India? What does its FIU-IND registration actually mean? What KYC does it require? How can an Indian user fund an account? Can crypto be withdrawn to a personal wallet? Who handles TDS? And what records should you keep for tax? These questions should not be collapsed into one answer.

The practical way to think about Binance in India is a sequence: Access → KYC → Funding → Trading → Withdrawal → Tax records. Each stage has its own rules and risks.

Is Binance available in India in 2026?

Yes. Binance currently operates an India-facing website and app for eligible users. Binance announced in August 2024 that it had registered as a Reporting Entity with the Financial Intelligence Unit – India (FIU-IND), and described the registration as part of its compliance with India’s anti-money-laundering framework. (Binance) FIU-IND’s own 2024 order also describes Binance as a Virtual Digital Asset Service Provider operating as a Reporting Entity under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act framework. (FIU-IND)

That distinction matters:

  • FIU registration does mean Binance has reporting and AML/KYC obligations under the relevant Indian framework.
  • FIU registration does not mean an RBI banking licence, deposit insurance, SEBI approval of every crypto product, government endorsement of crypto investing, or a guarantee that every Binance service is available in India.

So avoid both extremes — “Binance is illegal in India” and “Binance is fully government-approved”. Neither accurately describes the position.

Do Indian users need KYC on Binance?

Yes. Binance currently requires new users to complete the applicable identity verification before accessing its products and services, including crypto deposits and trading. Binance reiterated this requirement in a July 2026 update to its identity-verification policy. (Binance)

But KYC should not be described as one fixed India-wide checklist. The live verification process can vary according to residence, document-issuing country, verification level, payment route and account circumstances. Binance’s current flow asks users to select their residence, document country and document type, submit an accepted identity document, complete a liveness check and confirm their personal information — with additional verification possible depending on the account or payment channel. (Binance)

Why can KYC fail?

KYC rejection is often less dramatic than users assume. Common causes include blurry document images, expired documents, name or date mismatch, incomplete document edges, an unsupported document type, facial verification failure, or conflicting residence information. Binance says submitted identity information must match the supporting documents, and notes that review can take longer in particular cases. (Binance)

If verification fails, do not create multiple accounts simply to bypass the issue. Use the verification or identity-update route inside Binance. Binance currently provides a specific identity-update/reset process for users whose residence includes India. (Binance)

Can you deposit INR directly into Binance?

This is one area where articles become outdated very quickly. Do not treat “Binance India = P2P only” as a permanent rule. Binance supports several funding models globally — including P2P, cards, bank-related fiat routes and crypto deposits — but Binance itself states that fiat options vary by region. (Binance) For Indian users, P2P is an important INR-linked route, and Binance currently maintains active P2P functionality and India-facing P2P documentation. (Binance)

The correct rule is: check the funding and payment methods displayed in your own verified Binance account at the time of the transaction. Do not assume an INR route exists simply because Binance shows INR prices, another user has a payment option, an old tutorial shows UPI, or P2P was previously available in a certain format. Payment methods and counterparties can change.

How Binance P2P works

With P2P, you are generally buying or selling against another participant rather than depositing INR into a normal exchange fiat balance. A typical purchase flow is:

  1. Open P2P.
  2. Select the fiat currency and crypto.
  3. Choose an available seller/merchant.
  4. Review price, limits and payment method.
  5. Make payment according to the order instructions.
  6. Mark payment as completed only after actually paying.
  7. Wait for the seller to release the crypto.

Binance’s current P2P documentation confirms that local payment methods can be selected from those available in the platform, and that crypto is released after the counterparty confirms payment. (Binance)

P2P: the price matters more than “zero fee”

A P2P order may show no conventional spot trading fee. That does not automatically make it cheaper. Suppose the broader USDT market reference is ₹90 and a P2P seller quotes ₹91.80 — the economic difference is already 2%. So compare INR paid ÷ crypto actually received rather than looking only for a line labelled “fee”. The relevant variables include advertiser price, payment method, merchant limits, market demand and counterparty terms.

Never release crypto based on a screenshot

If you are selling through P2P, verify the actual money in your own bank/payment account. Do not rely solely on payment screenshots, WhatsApp messages, SMS screenshots or claims that the transfer is “processing”. Binance provides an appeal mechanism when P2P orders are disputed, but an appeal should not replace basic payment verification. (Binance)

Does Binance support crypto withdrawals in India?

Binance provides blockchain deposit and withdrawal functionality for supported assets and networks. Its current deposit guidance requires users to select the asset and network, and warns that blockchain confirmation time depends on the network. (Binance) But never write “every Binance user can withdraw every coin at any time”. Withdrawal availability can vary by asset, network, wallet maintenance, account status, jurisdiction and compliance review. Before buying an asset specifically to move it elsewhere, check the live withdrawal screen.

Network choice matters more than the withdrawal fee

Suppose Binance offers several USDT networks. Do not choose the cheapest one first. Use this order:

  1. Open the receiving wallet or exchange.
  2. Check which USDT networks it accepts.
  3. Return to Binance.
  4. Choose the exact same supported network.
  5. Compare the current withdrawal charge.
  6. Verify the address.
  7. Check memo/tag where required.
  8. Send a test amount when practical.

For detailed troubleshooting, see Crypto Transfer Checklist and USDT TRC20 vs ERC20 vs BEP20.

Binance withdrawal fee vs blockchain fee

These are not necessarily identical. An exchange can set its own withdrawal fee, while the underlying blockchain separately has its own network-cost mechanism. For example, Ethereum transactions use ETH for network gas, BNB Smart Chain uses BNB, and TRON uses its network resource/fee model. That is why we do not publish a permanent table claiming “Binance ERC20 = X USDT forever” — use the current withdrawal preview instead.

What about Travel Rule checks?

Binance updated its Travel Rule guidance in June 2026. It states that crypto deposits and withdrawals involving certain jurisdictions can require information about the originator and beneficiary, including names and wallet-related information. (Binance) That means a withdrawal flow can now involve more than address + network, depending on the jurisdiction and receiving platform — worth mentioning because many old Binance tutorials do not include it.

Binance fees: measure the full route

Do not compare Binance with CoinDCX or CoinSwitch using only one spot-fee percentage. Use Total Cost = Entry Cost + Trading Cost + Spread + Withdrawal Cost + Exit Cost. For example, if you start with ₹10,000, ask: how much INR did I spend? How much BTC/USDT did I actually receive? Was there a trading fee? What was the spread? What does withdrawal cost? How much reaches the destination wallet? This gives a more useful answer than “Binance fee = 0.X%”. See Crypto Exchange Fees in India.

Does Binance deduct Indian crypto TDS automatically?

Do not answer this with one universal sentence. The responsibility depends on the transaction structure. For transactions governed by the older Income-tax Act framework, Section 194S states that the person responsible for paying consideration to a resident for the transfer of a VDA generally deducts 1% TDS, subject to the applicable threshold and transaction structure. (Income Tax Department) The Income Tax Department gives several examples:

  • Direct buyer and seller / OTC-style transaction — the buyer is responsible for deduction.
  • Exchange transaction where the exchange pays the seller directly — the exchange can be responsible.
  • Broker-assisted exchange transaction — responsibility can involve the exchange or broker depending on the arrangement.
  • VDA-for-VDA transaction — both sides may create deduction obligations because each side is transferring a VDA. (Income Tax Department)

This is why “the seller deducts the 1% TDS on Binance P2P” is wrong, and “Binance always handles TDS” is also too broad. The correct question is: who is the payer, who is the resident seller, and how is the transaction structured?

Important 2026 tax-law transition

India moved to the Income Tax Act, 2025 from 1 April 2026 for income earned during Tax Year 2026-27 onwards. The new framework replaces the old “previous year / assessment year” terminology with “Tax Year”. (Income Tax Department) Under the new Act, the 1% VDA TDS rule appears under Section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 8(vi), with the same ₹50,000 / ₹10,000 threshold structure for relevant payers. (Income Tax Department) But if you are filing for AY 2026-27 = income earned in FY 2025-26, the old Income-tax Act, 1961 framework still applies. The Income Tax Department explicitly confirms this transitional treatment. (Income Tax Department) For the full explanation, see Crypto Tax in India.

What records should Binance users keep?

Do not rely only on a final annual P&L number. Keep trade history, deposits, withdrawals, P2P orders, transaction hashes, INR payment records, fees, TDS records where applicable, and wallet addresses you control. This becomes especially important if crypto moves between Binance → personal wallet → another exchange, because one platform may not know the original acquisition cost or the reason for the later transfer.

Is moving crypto from Binance to your own wallet taxable?

A transfer between accounts or wallets that you beneficially control is not automatically the same economic event as selling the asset. But do not treat every blockchain movement as identical — tax consequences can depend on what legally and economically occurred. Keep the TXID, amount, source wallet, destination wallet, network, fee, and evidence that both addresses/accounts belong to you. This makes later reconciliation easier.

Is Binance safer than self-custody?

That is the wrong comparison. Binance introduces platform custody, account-access dependency and withdrawal dependency. Self-custody introduces private-key risk, seed-phrase risk, transfer mistakes and malicious signing. The better question is which risks can you competently manage? See Crypto Exchange vs Self-Custody Wallet.

Security settings worth enabling

For a Binance account, consider a unique password, passkey or strong 2FA, a secure email, device/session review, an anti-phishing code, withdrawal whitelist where available, and limited API permissions. Do not hand over your OTP, password, seed phrase, private key, or remote desktop access.

Who may Binance suit?

Binance may be relevant to users who prioritise broad crypto-market access, multiple trading markets, blockchain withdrawals, P2P availability and international crypto infrastructure. It may be less suitable for someone who primarily wants the simplest INR-only workflow, a traditional Indian broker-style experience, or minimal network/custody decisions. That is why there is no useful universal answer to “is Binance the best exchange in India?” — compare it against the route you actually need.

Bottom line

Binance can be used by eligible users in India, and Binance is registered as an FIU-IND Reporting Entity. (Binance) But FIU registration, KYC, INR access, crypto withdrawals and tax are five different questions. Do not reduce Binance India to “P2P only”, do not assume “Binance handles every TDS obligation”, and do not treat KYC as one permanent PAN/Aadhaar checklist. The safest approach is: check the live account for platform features, use primary Indian tax sources for tax rules, and keep independent records of what you actually did.
