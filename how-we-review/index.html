How We Review

Last updated: 21 September 2026

CoinRaahi publishes practical guides on cryptocurrency, exchanges, stablecoins, wallets, crypto tax, US stocks, gold and tokenised assets for readers in India. Because these topics involve money, regulation and rules that change quickly, every guide goes through a structured review before it is published or updated.

The goal is simple: keep three things clearly separate — what is officially documented, what we observed ourselves, and what depends on the reader's own account, location or circumstances.

Our review process

Most guides move through the following steps.

1. Start with the user's question

Every article begins with a specific question — for example, can Indian users still use Binance, how do you buy USDT with INR, does 1% TDS apply, or what happens when a withdrawal is stuck. We answer that question directly before adding background, so the practical answer is never buried.

2. Check primary sources first

Wherever possible we use original material rather than another site's summary. Depending on the topic that may include the Income Tax Department, FIU-IND, RBI, SEBI, Indian legislation, official exchange help centres and fee schedules, wallet documentation, and blockchain explorers. Secondary sources may add context, but important claims are confirmed against a primary source.

3. Check India-specific conditions

Crypto platforms often work differently in different countries, so we do not assume a global feature is available in India. Where relevant we check INR payment options, Indian KYC, FIU-IND registration, TDS treatment, P2P access, supported banking methods and withdrawal availability. If the answer depends on the account or location, we say so.

4. Test the process where practical

Where practical, we test part of a process first-hand — registration screens, KYC flows, P2P interfaces, deposit and withdrawal options, network selection and fee displays. When a guide includes hands-on testing, we state when the test was performed and separate what we observed from what the platform's documentation says should happen. Interfaces, fees and features can change and may differ by account.

5. Verify numbers and fees

Fees are one of the easiest things to get wrong. For cost-related guides we check trading, deposit and withdrawal fees, network fees, spreads, P2P price differences, minimums and tax deductions. We avoid calling a service "free" when a spread, network fee or tax can still apply.

6. Separate facts from estimates

We distinguish three kinds of information: confirmed (supported by an official source or a directly observable rule), observed (seen during our own testing), and examples (worked calculations used to explain how a fee or tax behaves). Examples are never presented as guaranteed results.

7. Extra review for tax and regulatory content

Tax and regulatory guides get additional care because they can affect real financial decisions. We prioritise primary sources and separate what the law says, what a platform does, and how a rule is commonly applied. CoinRaahi does not provide personalised tax or legal advice.

8. Review time-sensitive information

Fees, networks, KYC, INR payment methods, P2P rules, withdrawal limits and regulatory status all change. We show a publication or review date on important guides, and when something changes materially we update the article rather than leave outdated instructions live.

Review labels

You may see one or more of these labels on an article:

  • Official sources checked. Key factual claims have been reviewed against primary material where available.
  • India-specific review. The guide has been reviewed with Indian users, INR access, local rules and Indian tax implications in mind.
  • Hands-on tested. CoinRaahi directly tested at least part of the process described. The guide states when the test was carried out.
  • Human reviewed. The article was reviewed by a person before publication or after a material update.

How we review exchange comparisons

Comparisons are not reduced to a single score. We look across availability for Indian users, regulatory status, INR access, supported assets, fees, spreads, P2P options, custody, withdrawal experience and security. Different users weigh these differently, so we explain the trade-offs rather than declare one platform universally best.

Affiliate relationships

Some pages may contain affiliate links; if a reader registers through one, CoinRaahi may receive compensation. That does not determine which facts we publish, how fees are calculated, or whether risks are mentioned. See our Affiliate Disclosure.

Corrections

When we identify a material error — a wrong fee, an outdated tax rule, a changed withdrawal limit or an unavailable payment method — we correct it and, where appropriate, note the change. See our Corrections Policy.

What we do not do

CoinRaahi does not give personalised investment, financial, legal or tax advice, guarantee returns, or publish paid rankings disguised as independent comparisons. Crypto and tokenised assets carry financial, custody and regulatory risk.
