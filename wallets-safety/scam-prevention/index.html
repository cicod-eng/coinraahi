How to Spot Crypto Scams

Last updated: 6 September 2026

The most dangerous crypto scams do not always look technical. They often look ordinary: a WhatsApp message, a Telegram support account, a profitable trading group, a fake job, a payment screenshot, or a “recovery expert”. The scammer’s goal is usually to make you act before you verify.

Fake exchange support

A common pattern: a user posts about an account problem, a fake support account contacts them privately, and the scammer asks for login information or wallet recovery details. Support contacting you first is not proof that support is genuine. Use the official app or website.

WhatsApp and Telegram investment groups

Typical claims include guaranteed returns, insider signals, a 95% win rate, “institutional” account access and secret arbitrage. A screenshot of past profits proves very little. The strongest warning sign is pressure to transfer money quickly.

Remote-access scams

A scammer tells the user to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer or another remote-control app, claiming this is needed to fix KYC, unlock withdrawal or recover funds. Do not give strangers control of a device containing banking or crypto accounts.

P2P screenshot scams

A buyer sends an image showing “payment successful”. The seller releases crypto. The money never arrived. Verify your own bank account, not the buyer’s screenshot.

Fake payment reversal stories

A scammer may pressure you with “payment stuck”, “bank delay”, “release first” or “support told me you must confirm”. Do not release assets until your own payment status is clear.

Wallet-drainer websites

A site asks you to connect a wallet and sign a transaction. The signature may approve token spending, NFT transfers or unlimited allowance. Never treat a wallet signature as harmless because “no password was entered”.

Seed phrase phishing

No legitimate website needs your seed phrase just to verify a wallet, receive an airdrop, fix a transaction or connect to support. If a form asks for 12 or 24 recovery words, close it.

Fake recovery services

Scams often have a second stage. After someone loses money, another person claims they can recover the crypto for a fee. Real blockchain recovery is highly case-specific. Anyone promising guaranteed recovery deserves extreme caution.

Job/task scams

A victim is asked to complete tasks, rate products, deposit small amounts, unlock “commission” and pay more to withdraw. Crypto or USDT may be used as the payment rail. The scam is not made legitimate by using a real blockchain.

Fake tax or government notices

Scammers can exploit fear of tax, KYC, FIU and account freezing. Verify notices through official channels before sending money or documents.

The five-minute scam test

  1. Who contacted whom first?
  2. Can I verify this person independently?
  3. Why is there urgency?
  4. Why do they need crypto specifically?
  5. What happens if I refuse?

Legitimate services normally survive five minutes of verification. Scams often depend on stopping you from checking.

If you think you have been scammed

Immediately stop further payments, secure email and exchange accounts, revoke wallet approvals if relevant, save TXIDs and messages, contact the real platform, preserve bank/payment evidence, and report through appropriate official channels. Do not pay a second “recovery fee” without verification.

Frequently asked questions

Is a verified Telegram account safe?

A badge or professional-looking profile is not enough. Use the company's official support entry point.

Can a crypto payment screenshot be fake?

Yes. Verify the payment independently.

Can someone steal crypto just by knowing my wallet address?

Normally an address alone does not give control of the wallet. Private keys, seed phrases and malicious approvals are more sensitive.

Can a wallet signature be dangerous?

Yes. Some signatures authorise token permissions or transactions.

Can stolen crypto always be recovered?

No. Recovery depends on the transaction path and platform involvement.
