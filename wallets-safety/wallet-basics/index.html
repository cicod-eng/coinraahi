Crypto Wallet Basics: Who Controls the Keys?

Last updated: 6 September 2026

Almost everything about using crypto safely comes down to one question: who controls the private keys? If you hold them, you control the asset. If a platform holds them, you have a claim against the platform. The coin itself is not stored inside a wallet at all.

A wallet stores keys, not coins

The asset lives on the blockchain — a shared ledger that records balances and transfers. What a wallet actually holds is the private key that lets you authorise moves of a specific balance.

This is why losing a wallet file is not necessarily the problem. Losing the keys — or letting someone else get them — is the problem.

The custody question

There are two broad arrangements, and the difference is control, not convenience:

  • Self-custody: you generate and hold the keys. You are the only party who can move the asset — and the only one who can recover from a mistake.
  • Custodial (exchange or app): the platform holds the keys and gives you an account balance. Convenient, but you depend on the platform’s security and solvency.

Neither is automatically “right”. It depends on the amount involved, your technical comfort and how much you can manage the responsibility of safeguarding a seed phrase.

Where is the asset actually held?

The asset is recorded on the blockchain, not “inside” an app. The app is a window. When you move crypto from an exchange to a wallet, what actually moves is control: the balance is reassigned to an address only your key can authorise.

Seed phrase leakedAnyone with it can move the funds

Wrong network or addressTransfers can be stuck or unrecoverable
Exchange / platform failureCustodial balances depend on the platform
Device compromiseKeys stored online are exposed to malware

The seed phrase is the real asset

A seed phrase (also called a recovery phrase) can regenerate every key in a self-custody wallet. Protect it like the balance it controls:

  • Write it on paper or metal, not in a note app or screenshot.
  • Store it offline, away from the device you use daily.
  • Never enter it on a website, and never share it with “support”.

Choosing by your situation

Rather than picking a “best wallet”, start from what you actually need:

  • Active trading: you may keep working balances on an exchange for liquidity, accepting custodial risk on that amount.
  • Longer-term holding: self-custody shifts control to you, but also shifts responsibility for the seed phrase to you.
  • Frequent small transfers: a software wallet can be practical, with care around the device it runs on.
  • Larger, rarely-moved holdings: some users separate them from internet-connected devices entirely.

There is no single answer — only a set of trade-offs around control, convenience and responsibility.

Frequently asked questions

Do I own my crypto if it is held on an exchange?

You hold an account balance, while the platform controls the keys and the underlying custody. It is a custodial arrangement, different from self-custody.

What is a seed phrase?

A recovery phrase that can regenerate your private keys. Anyone who obtains it can generally control the wallet, so it should never be shared or stored online.

What happens if I lose my seed phrase?

You may permanently lose access to the wallet. There is no central authority that can recover a self-custody wallet's keys.

Are hardware wallets safer than software wallets?

They can reduce certain online risks by keeping keys off an internet-connected device, but security still depends on how the seed phrase is stored and used.

What is the difference between a wallet and an exchange?

A self-custody wallet gives you the private keys; an exchange holds the keys on your behalf and gives you an account balance.
