Crypto Transfers: What to Check Before You Send

Last updated: 6 September 2026

Most crypto transfers are not lost by the network — they are lost at the moment of sending, by missing one checkable detail: the network, the address, a required memo, the minimum, or the fee. This page is a pre-send checklist and a what-to-do guide for when a transfer does not arrive.

The pre-send checklist

  1. Network — do both ends support the same token on the same network?
  2. Address — is it complete, correct and for the right network?
  3. Memo / tag — does the destination require one?
  4. Minimum — does the amount meet the destination’s minimum?
  5. Fee — do you know the exchange fee plus any network cost?

Network: the most common mistake

The same asset can exist on more than one network. USDT, for example, runs on Tron (TRC-20), Ethereum (ERC-20), BNB Chain (BEP-20) and others. Sending USDT on one network to an address or wallet set up for a different one can put the funds out of reach.

Addresses can look similar across networks — both Ethereum and BNB Chain addresses start with “0x”. The address format alone does not tell you the network. Always match the network on both the sending and receiving side.

Address: verify, don’t glance

Copy-paste the full address and then check the first and last several characters. Clipboard-swapping malware can silently replace a copied address with an attacker’s. Sending to the wrong address is often irreversible.

Memo, tag and destination tag

Some networks and many exchange deposit flows use a memo or destination tag to route a transfer to the right account at a shared address. If a memo is required and you omit it, the funds may not credit to your account even though the transaction succeeds on-chain.

Minimum and fee

Sending below a destination’s minimum deposit can leave funds uncredited. And the amount that arrives is the sent amount minus the network cost, so a tiny transfer can be swallowed by fees. The fee you pay at an exchange is the exchange’s withdrawal fee plus any network cost — both vary.

What to do when a transfer goes wrong

SituationFirst thing to do
PendingCheck the transaction ID on a block explorer; wait for confirmations
Not receivedConfirm network, address and memo first, then check the transaction ID
Wrong networkContact official support with the transaction ID; recovery is not guaranteed
Memo missingContact the destination’s official support with the transaction ID
Fee looks highSeparate the exchange’s fee from the network cost

Use the transaction ID as your evidence in every case. Legitimate support channels will ask for it; scammers may ask for your seed phrase instead — never share that.

Test transfers

For a large amount, a new address, or an unfamiliar network, send a small test amount first and confirm it arrives before sending the rest. The extra fee is cheap insurance against an unrecoverable mistake.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check before sending crypto?

Network compatibility between the two ends, the full address, any required memo or tag, the minimum amount and the total fee.

Why has my transfer not arrived?

It may still be pending on the network, or the destination may need additional confirmations. Check the transaction ID on a block explorer.

What if I sent crypto on the wrong network?

Recovery may or may not be possible depending on the platform and the receiving wallet. Contact official support and provide the transaction ID.

Do I need a memo or destination tag?

Only on certain networks and exchange deposits that require one. Check the destination's instructions before sending.

Should I send a test transfer first?

For a large amount or a new address, sending a small test amount first is generally sensible.
