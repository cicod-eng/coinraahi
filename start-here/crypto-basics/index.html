Crypto Basics for Beginners in India

Last updated: 6 September 2026

Crypto becomes much easier once you stop trying to learn everything at once. A beginner does not need to understand smart contracts, DeFi, staking and tokenisation on day one.

Start with six questions:

  1. What is the asset?
  2. Where is it recorded?
  3. Where can you buy or access it?
  4. Who controls it after you buy?
  5. How do you move it?
  6. What can go wrong?

This page gives you the learning order CoinRaahi recommends.

Start with the asset, not the app

Many beginners start by downloading an exchange. That puts the steps in the wrong order. Before using a platform, understand what you are buying.

  • Bitcoin — a blockchain-based asset with no central company issuing it.
  • Ethereum — a blockchain network that supports smart contracts, tokens and applications. ETH is the network’s native asset.
  • Stablecoins — tokens such as USDT and USDC designed to track a reference currency such as the US dollar.

These three categories already behave very differently.

What does a blockchain actually do?

A blockchain is a shared record of transactions maintained by a network rather than one central database operator.

For beginners, the useful concepts are transaction, block, confirmation, network, address, fee and explorer. You do not need to understand cryptography mathematics before making sense of crypto.

What is a crypto exchange?

An exchange is a platform that helps users buy and sell crypto, convert between assets, hold balances, and deposit or withdraw funds. But an exchange and a blockchain are not the same thing. If crypto stays inside a centralised exchange, the exchange normally manages custody.

What is a wallet?

A crypto wallet manages the credentials used to control blockchain assets. There are two broad models.

  • Custodial: a platform manages the keys.
  • Self-custody: you control the wallet credentials.

Neither is automatically right for every beginner. The important question is: who can recover access if something goes wrong?

Networks matter during transfers

A token can exist on more than one blockchain. For example, USDT can be transferred through different supported networks. Before sending crypto, check:

asset → network → address → memo/tag → minimum → fee

Choosing the cheapest network without checking recipient support is a common mistake.

Crypto prices are only one type of risk

Beginners often think the main risk is that the price may fall. There are several others: exchange risk, custody risk, phishing, wrong-network transfers, stablecoin issuer risk, smart-contract risk and scam risk. Learning operational safety matters before increasing the amount you use.

India-specific concepts beginners should know

Crypto use in India also involves local considerations such as KYC, PAN-related account workflows, VDA tax rules, TDS under applicable conditions, and INR entry and exit routes. Do not assume that “crypto is taxed” means every wallet movement produces the same tax result.

What you do not need to learn yet

A complete beginner can safely postpone leverage, perpetual futures, options, liquidity mining, complex bridges, yield strategies and obscure altcoins. Learning fewer things properly is more useful than learning fifty terms without understanding custody or transfers.

A simple learning order

StageLearn this first
1Bitcoin, Ethereum, stablecoins
2Exchanges and KYC
3Wallets and custody
4Networks and transfers
5Security and scams
6India tax basics
7RWA, DeFi and advanced products

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a lot of money to learn crypto?

No. Learning the concepts does not require buying anything, and platform minimums vary.

Should I learn Bitcoin before altcoins?

It is usually useful to understand Bitcoin, Ethereum and stablecoins before moving to less established assets.

Do I need a wallet immediately?

Not necessarily. First understand the difference between exchange custody and self-custody.

Is crypto the same as blockchain?

No. Blockchain is infrastructure; crypto-assets are one type of thing that can operate on blockchain networks.

Is USDT the same as USD?

No. USDT is a crypto token designed to track the US dollar.
